Texas is home to more active-duty service members than almost any other state, with major posts from Fort Hood to Joint Base San Antonio to Fort Bliss. If your orders point you to the Lone Star State, there is good news beyond the barbecue: Texas VA loan benefits stack unusually well. On top of the federal VA (Department of Veterans Affairs) loan, Texas adds no state income tax, some of the strongest veteran property tax exemptions in the country, and its own home loan program for veterans. Used together, these can save a military family real money for as long as they own the home. Here is how the pieces fit.

## The Federal VA Loan, in Plain English

Start with the benefit you already earned. A VA loan lets eligible service members and veterans buy a primary home with no down payment and no private mortgage insurance, usually at competitive rates. You pay a one-time VA funding fee, which is waived if you have a service-connected disability rating. If you are new to the program, read [what are the benefits of a VA loan](/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-a-va-loan) and [how a $0 down VA loan works](/blog/how-does-a-0-down-va-loan-work) first.

Everything below is what Texas adds on top of that federal foundation.

## Texas Benefit 1: No State Income Tax

Texas is one of a handful of states with no state income tax. For a military family, that means your pay, and a working spouse's pay, are not taxed by the state. Over a three-year tour, keeping that money can add up to thousands of dollars that a family in a high-tax state would never see. It is a quiet benefit, but it is one reason Texas stretches a military paycheck further.

## Texas Benefit 2: Veteran Property Tax Exemptions

Texas has no state income tax, so it leans more on property taxes, which run higher than the national average. That is exactly why the state's veteran property tax exemptions matter so much. They come in two forms.

For veterans with a partial disability rating, the state offers a fixed exemption off a property's value, based on the rating.

| VA disability rating | Exemption up to |
|---|---|
| 10% to 29% | $5,000 |
| 30% to 49% | $7,500 |
| 50% to 69% | $10,000 |
| 70% to 100% | $12,000 |

*Source: [Texas Comptroller disabled veteran exemption FAQ](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/disabledvet-faq.php), Tax Code Section 11.22.*

The bigger benefit is for veterans rated 100 percent disabled. Under [Tax Code Section 11.131](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/disabledvet-100-faq.php), a veteran who receives 100 percent compensation from the VA due to a 100 percent disability rating or a determination of individual unemployability pays no property tax at all on their residence homestead. A surviving spouse of a service member killed in the line of duty can also qualify for a total homestead exemption.

## Texas Benefit 3: The Texas Vet Loan

Texas runs its own home loan program through the Texas Veterans Land Board, part of the General Land Office. Often called the Texas Vet Loan, its Veterans Housing Assistance Program offers fixed-rate home loans for a veteran's primary residence in Texas. According to the [Texas General Land Office](https://www.glo.texas.gov/veterans/home-loans), veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 30 percent or more can receive an added 0.5 percent discount on the rate, and the program is self-funded through bonds rather than taxpayer dollars.

A Texas Vet Loan is a separate program from the federal VA loan, and a local lender can help you compare the two and choose the better fit for your rate, term, and price range.

## Putting the Benefits Together

Imagine a staff sergeant with a 100 percent disability rating buying a $300,000 home near Fort Hood. The VA loan means no down payment and no funding fee. Texas charges no state income tax on the family's pay. And because of the 100 percent disabled veteran exemption, the family owes no property tax on the home. Stacked together, those benefits can free up hundreds of dollars a month compared with the same purchase in a high-tax state.

Your exact savings depend on your rating, your price, and the county you buy in, but the pattern holds: Texas is one of the friendliest states in the country for a veteran homebuyer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use a VA loan and a Texas veteran property tax exemption together?**
Yes. The VA loan is a federal home-financing benefit, and the property tax exemptions are separate Texas benefits. Eligible veterans can use both on the same home.

**Do I have to be a Texas resident to get the exemptions?**
The disabled veteran homestead exemptions apply to your Texas residence homestead. Once the home is your primary residence, you can apply through your county appraisal district.

**Is the Texas Vet Loan the same as a VA loan?**
No. The Texas Vet Loan is a state program through the Veterans Land Board. It is separate from the federal VA loan, though both serve veterans. A local lender can compare them for you.
