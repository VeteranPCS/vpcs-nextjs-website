The list of army bases in North Carolina is shorter than most people expect. There is one very large post, a handful of smaller Army-run sites, and a lot of confusion about names. If you have orders here, this guide sorts all of it out in one place.

We will cover each Army installation, what it does, the Fort Bragg name change that happened twice, and where families settle once they arrive. PCS is short for Permanent Change of Station, the military term for an assigned move.

## The Army Bases in North Carolina, One by One

North Carolina has one active-duty Army post: Fort Bragg. Everything else on the Army side is either part of Fort Bragg, a specialized Army facility, or a National Guard or Army Reserve site.

| Installation | Nearest city | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Fort Bragg | Fayetteville | The state's only active-duty Army post. Home of XVIII Airborne Corps, the 82nd Airborne Division, and U.S. Army Special Operations Command |
| Pope Army Airfield | Fayetteville (on Fort Bragg) | The airfield on Fort Bragg. Air Force airlift units are tenants here |
| Camp Mackall | Hoffman, near Southern Pines | A Fort Bragg training subinstallation used for Special Forces assessment and selection |
| Military Ocean Terminal Sunny Point | Southport | An Army-run ammunition port. The nation's largest, and the Army's main East Coast deep-water ammunition terminal |
| Army Research Office | Research Triangle Park, near Durham | The Army's basic research office. An office, not a housing installation |
| Camp Butner Training Center | Stem, near Durham | North Carolina Army National Guard training center and rifle ranges |
| Joint Force Headquarters, North Carolina National Guard | Raleigh | Headquarters for the state's Army and Air National Guard |
| 108th Training Command | Charlotte | An Army Reserve headquarters that runs drill sergeant and initial-entry training |

*One active-duty post, three other Army-run sites, and the Guard and Reserve locations. Source: North Carolina Department of Military and Veterans Affairs and U.S. Army Special Operations Command.*

Two things are worth calling out. Pope Army Airfield and Camp Mackall are not separate assignments the way people assume. Both belong to Fort Bragg, so your housing allowance and your house hunt follow Fort Bragg. Camp Butner and the Raleigh headquarters are National Guard, so soldiers there usually already live in the area.

## Fort Bragg, Fort Liberty, Then Fort Bragg Again

Here it is plainly. The post was named Fort Bragg for more than a century. In 2023 it was renamed Fort Liberty. On February 14, 2025, the Army announced that [Fort Liberty was renamed Fort Bragg, effective immediately](https://www.army.mil/article/283111/fort_liberty_is_renamed_fort_bragg_effective_immediately). A redesignation ceremony followed in March 2025.

The official explanation for the 2025 change is that the name now honors Pfc. Roland L. Bragg, a World War II paratrooper who earned a Silver Star at the Battle of the Bulge, along with every soldier and family who has called the post home. Same spelling as before, tied to a different person.

What this means for you is simple. The current, correct name is Fort Bragg. You may still see Fort Liberty on older orders, school records, utility accounts, and map apps. Same place, same gates. If a form says Fort Liberty, it is not worth chasing down.

## Fort Bragg by the Numbers

Fort Bragg is the reason North Carolina shows up on almost every "biggest Army base" list. According to [U.S. Army Special Operations Command](https://www.soc.mil/NewComers/FortBragg.html), the installation covers about 161,000 acres, or roughly 251 square miles, and stretches into six counties. More than 55,000 service members and about 12,000 civilian employees work there, with roughly 25,000 family members living on post.

The major commands there include XVIII Airborne Corps, the 82nd Airborne Division, U.S. Army Special Operations Command, and Joint Special Operations Command. In practice that means a high operational tempo. The 82nd keeps a brigade on short-notice recall at all times, so alerts are a normal part of life here in a way they are not at most stateside posts.

## Where Fort Bragg Families Actually Live

Fort Bragg is big enough that "near post" means very different things depending on which gate you use. Here is an honest look at the main options.

### Close to the gates

Fayetteville is the largest city next to post and has the widest range of price points, from older brick ranches to newer builds on the west side. It is the shortest commute for most work areas and the easiest place to find something in a 30-day window. The trade-off is that school quality varies a lot by neighborhood, so tour the specific attendance zone instead of trusting a city-wide rating.

Spring Lake sits on the north side and is convenient to the 82nd Airborne footprint. Homes are generally cheaper and the commute is short. It also carries a weaker school reputation than areas south of post, which is the main reason families skip it.

### The middle ring

Hope Mills and Raeford are the most common landing spots for families who want more house and better-rated schools without a long drive. Both are a modest step up in commute from Fayetteville, depending on your gate and the time of day. Raeford, in Hoke County, has a lot of newer construction. Hope Mills leans more established.

Sanford is farther north and suits families who want acreage, or who have a spouse commuting toward Raleigh. The drive is longer and the two-lane approaches can be unpredictable.

### Worth the drive

Southern Pines and Pinehurst, in Moore County, are the quiet end of the market. Golf, good schools, a slower pace, higher prices. Expect the longest commute of the group. If you are on a staff schedule that works well. If your unit holds early morning formations, drive it once at 5:30 a.m. before you commit.

## Buying or Renting on a Fort Bragg BAH

BAH is Basic Allowance for Housing, the tax-free monthly amount the military pays toward your housing. Fort Bragg and Pope Army Airfield sit in Military Housing Area NC182, and the rate depends on your rank and whether you have dependents.

For 2026, the monthly rate with dependents is $1,806 for an E-5, $2,094 for an E-7, and $2,175 for an O-3. Rates changed on January 1, 2026, and you should confirm your own figure with the [Department of Defense BAH rate lookup](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/BAH-Rate-Lookup/) before you build a budget around it.

Set that against the local market. The typical home value in Fayetteville was $225,510 as of July 31, 2026, essentially flat over the prior year, per the [Zillow Home Value Index](https://www.zillow.com/home-values/52001/fayetteville-nc/). That is a typical home value, not a median sale price. The same page put Fayetteville's average asking rent at $1,368 that month.

*Fort Bragg is one of the few large posts where BAH still covers a typical local payment with room left over.*

That gap is why buying comes up so often here. A few honest guardrails.

- Rent first if this is a two-year tour, or if a deployment lands inside year one. Selling on a short timeline is where military owners lose money.
- Buy if you expect three or more years, have stable income, and can absorb a slow resale.
- Run the real payment, not just principal and interest. Taxes, insurance, and any homeowners association dues come out of the same allowance.
- Get fully underwritten preapproval before you arrive, not a quick prequalification. It shortens closing when your storage clock is running.

One more thing worth knowing if you are staying in the state after service. North Carolina excludes the first $45,000 of assessed value on a primary residence for qualifying disabled veterans and certain surviving spouses, under state law. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many Army bases are in North Carolina?

There is one active-duty Army post, Fort Bragg. Three other Army-run sites sit in the state: Pope Army Airfield and Camp Mackall, both part of Fort Bragg, and Military Ocean Terminal Sunny Point at Southport. The Army Research Office near Durham, Camp Butner Training Center, the National Guard headquarters in Raleigh, and the 108th Training Command in Charlotte round out the Army footprint.

### Is Fort Bragg still called Fort Liberty?

No. The post was Fort Liberty from 2023 until February 2025, when the Army renamed it Fort Bragg, effective immediately. The current official name is Fort Bragg. Older paperwork and some map apps may still say Fort Liberty.

### What is the biggest Army base in North Carolina?

Fort Bragg, by a wide margin. It covers about 161,000 acres across six counties and supports more than 55,000 service members, making it one of the largest Army installations in the world by population.

### Which city do most Fort Bragg soldiers live in?

Fayetteville holds the most military families, followed by Spring Lake, Hope Mills, and Raeford. Southern Pines and Pinehurst draw families willing to trade a longer commute for a quieter setting.

### Is Pope Army Airfield a separate base?

No. Pope became part of Fort Bragg in 2011 under base realignment. Air Force airlift units operate there as tenants, but the housing area, gates, and services are Fort Bragg's.
