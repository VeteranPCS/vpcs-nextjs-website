Not every set of orders lines up with summer. If your report date lands in the cold months, you are looking at a winter PCS move, and that is not a bad thing. A PCS (Permanent Change of Station) move in the off-season can actually go smoother than a summer one, as long as you plan for the weather. This guide covers what changes when you move in winter and how to make it work for your family.

The biggest surprise for most families is that winter can be the calmer time to move. Let's start there.

## **Why Off-Season Moves Have Real Upsides**

Summer is the crunch. About 300,000 service members and families move each year, and roughly 120,000 of those moves happen during the mid-May to September peak, according to the [U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/256310/pcs_season_is_coming_plan_ahead). That rush competes with civilian summer moves, which stretches moving crews thin and fills up rental trucks and hotels.

Winter flips that. When you move in the off-season, moving companies have more open dates, more flexibility, and often more time to handle your belongings with care. You may find it easier to lock in the pack-out and delivery days you want, and temporary lodging near your new base is usually easier to book. Fewer families competing for the same crews can mean a less rushed experience overall.

## **Prepare Your Belongings for the Cold**

Cold and moisture are hard on household goods, so pack with the weather in mind.

- Keep a clearly labeled cold-weather essentials box with coats, gloves, hats, and blankets that travels with you, not on the truck.
- Protect electronics, instruments, and anything with liquid or batteries. Extreme cold can crack screens and drain power, so carry the sensitive items with you when you can.
- Wipe down anything that might collect condensation when it moves from a cold truck into a warm home, and let boxes adjust to room temperature before you unpack electronics.
- Make sure walkways at both homes are clear of ice and snow on moving day. Movers can refuse to work an unsafe path, and a fall can end your move day fast.

Remember that some items are on the do-not-pack list no matter the season. Our guide to [what military movers will not pack](/blog/prepare-to-pcs-what-military-movers-wont-pack-in-2024) covers the flammables and hazards to set aside before pack-out.

## **Winterize Your Travel**

If you are driving to your next duty station, treat the road trip as its own project. Winter weather and short daylight hours change how you plan the drive.

- Service your vehicle first. Check the battery, tires, antifreeze, wiper blades, and heater before you leave.
- Build a car emergency kit with a blanket, flashlight, water, snacks, a phone charger, and a small bag of sand or kitty litter for traction.
- Watch the forecast along your whole route, not just your start and end points. A clear morning can turn into an icy pass by afternoon.
- Plan shorter driving days. With less daylight and possible weather delays, give yourself margin so you are never forced to push through a storm.

The federal government's winter driving safety tips from [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/winter-weather) are a quick, useful refresher before a long cold-weather drive.

## **Mind the Holiday Calendar**

Winter moves often bump into the holidays, and that affects scheduling. Transportation offices, housing offices, and many businesses run on reduced hours or close entirely around late December. Moving crews book up fast in the days right before and after a holiday because their own staff want time off, too.

Start your arrangements early and confirm your dates in writing. If your move falls near a holiday, ask your transportation office how the calendar affects your pack-out and delivery windows. Building in a few extra days of buffer keeps a closed office or a snow day from derailing your whole timeline. ## **Lodging, Kids, and Pets in Winter**

A winter arrival can mean a longer stretch in temporary housing while you find a permanent place, since fewer homes list in the cold months. [Temporary Lodging Expense](/blog/temporary-lodging-expense-tle-pcs-guide), or TLE, helps cover those hotel days near your new base, so know your allowance before you go.

Moving mid-school-year adds another layer for families with children. Reach out to the new school early, gather records, and give kids time to adjust. Our [PCS with kids tips](/blog/pcs-with-kids-10-sanity-saving-tips-every-military-family-needs-to-know) are built for exactly this kind of transition. Traveling with animals in the cold takes planning as well, and our guide to a [PCS move with pets](/blog/navigating-your-pcs-move-with-pets-a-comprehensive-guide) covers the essentials.

## **Start Your Housing Search Early**

Winter inventory is thinner, so give yourself a head start on finding a home. If your orders allow it, [house-hunting leave](/blog/house-hunting-leave-pcs-guide) lets you scout your new area in person before you commit. Working with a local agent who knows the market near your base is even more valuable in the off-season, when good listings move quickly and you may be shopping from far away.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

**Is it better to PCS in winter or summer?**

Both work, but winter has real advantages. Because most military moves happen in the summer, off-season movers usually find more open dates, easier truck rentals, and simpler lodging. The trade-off is weather, so you plan for cold and snow instead of crowds.

**What should I pack separately for a winter move?**

Keep a cold-weather essentials box with you: coats, gloves, hats, and blankets, plus sensitive electronics and anything with batteries or liquids that cold could damage. That box should travel in your vehicle, not on the moving truck.

**Do moving companies operate in winter?**

Yes. Crews run year-round, and winter is often less booked than summer. Just start early and confirm dates in writing, especially around the holidays when offices and crews take time off.

## **Land Your Winter Move With Confidence**

A winter PCS move rewards families who plan ahead. Handle the weather, mind the holiday calendar, and start your housing search early, and the off-season can be the easiest time to move.
