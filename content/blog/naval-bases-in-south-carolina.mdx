If you are looking for naval bases in South Carolina, you may be surprised. The state's big stand-alone naval base closed in 1996. But the Navy never left. Thousands of Sailors still train in Goose Creek every year, the Navy runs a major engineering center in North Charleston, and a Navy hospital cares for Marines and recruits in Beaufort. This guide covers each Navy site, the towns nearby, and what 2026 BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing) looks like.

## What Happened to the Charleston Naval Base?

For most of the 1900s, Charleston was a Navy town. The Charleston Naval Shipyard built and repaired ships on the Cooper River. That changed with BRAC (Base Realignment and Closure), the process Congress uses to close or merge bases. Per the [Navy BRAC office page on the former shipyard](https://www.bracpmo.navy.mil/BRAC-Bases/Southeast/Former-Naval-Shipyard-Charleston/), the base was picked for closure in 1993 and shut down on April 1, 1996.

The old site did not sit empty. Today it hosts the Coast Guard, NOAA, a federal law enforcement training center, and the lab working on the H.L. Hunley Civil War submarine.

## Naval Support Activity Charleston (the Weapons Station)

The Navy's main home in South Carolina is the old Naval Weapons Station, north of Charleston. Per the [Navy's NSA Charleston history page](https://cnrse.cnic.navy.mil/Installations/NSA-Charleston/About/History/), it opened on November 5, 1941, as an ammunition depot. It started with 6,368 acres. Today it covers more than 17,000 acres and has:

- More than 11,000 military, civilian and contractor workers.
- More than 40 tenant commands.
- More than 1,800 homes on base.

In 2010, the Weapons Station and the nearby Air Force base merged into Joint Base Charleston. The Air Force's 628th Air Base Wing now runs the joint base, per the [Military OneSource Joint Base Charleston overview](https://installations.militaryonesource.mil/in-depth-overview/joint-base-charleston). You will hear both names. The Navy still calls its side "NSA Charleston."

The base's [official units list](https://www.jbcharleston.jb.mil/Units/) includes these Navy commands:

- Naval Nuclear Power Training Command.
- Nuclear Power Training Unit Charleston.
- Naval Information Warfare Center Atlantic.
- Naval Health Clinic Charleston.
- Navy Munitions Command.
- Naval Consolidated Brig Charleston, a Navy confinement facility.

Army units also work here, including the 841st Transportation Battalion. Read about them in our guide to [Army bases in South Carolina](/blog/army-bases-in-south-carolina).

## Nuclear Power School in Goose Creek

If you or your spouse are in the Navy's nuclear field, Goose Creek is probably on your orders. Naval Nuclear Power Training Command (NNPTC) teaches officers and enlisted Sailors how to run the nuclear plants that power submarines and aircraft carriers. Classes cover chemistry, physics, heat transfer and plant systems.

Per [NAVSEA's NNPTC history page](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/NNPTC/About/History/), the school moved from Orlando to Charleston between 1998 and 1999. The complex now has more than 3,600 students and 480 staff. Military OneSource says more than 3,000 Sailors graduate each year.

After the classroom, many students go next door to Nuclear Power Training Unit (NPTU) Charleston. There, they train hands-on aboard two moored training submarines. Per the [NPTU Charleston page](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/home/nptu/charleston), the unit also trains civilian engineers from the Navy's nuclear labs.

**A housing tip for students:** nuclear training is a set number of months, not a full tour. Many students rent. If you have a family and a longer follow-on tour in the area, buying can still make sense. Talk with a local agent before you decide.

## NIWC Atlantic in North Charleston

Naval Information Warfare Center (NIWC) Atlantic is the Navy's research and engineering center for information warfare. Think secure communications, networks and command systems. Per the [NIWC Atlantic About page](https://www.niwcatlantic.navy.mil/About), Charleston is its largest site. It has about 3,055 government employees and 19 military members there, in nearly 80 buildings.

Most of the workforce is civilian. If your spouse works in engineering or IT, NIWC Atlantic is one of the biggest employers in the area.

## Naval Hospital Beaufort

About 70 miles southwest of Charleston, Naval Hospital Beaufort serves the Marine Corps bases in Beaufort County. Per the [Naval Hospital Beaufort About Us page](https://beaufort.tricare.mil/About-Us), it opened in 1949, sits on 127 acres and serves about 35,000 beneficiaries. It runs clinics at MCRD (Marine Corps Recruit Depot) Parris Island and MCAS (Marine Corps Air Station) Beaufort.

The military health system has been reviewing which hospitals keep inpatient care. Before you move, check the hospital's website for the services it offers now. ## What BAH Covers Near Navy Sites in South Carolina

BAH is a monthly, tax-free payment to help cover housing off base. It depends on your pay grade, your duty ZIP code and whether you have dependents. Here are 2026 rates from the [DTMO BAH rate lookup](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/BAH-Rate-Lookup/). Each cell shows with dependents first, then without.

| Location | Rate area | E-5 | E-7 | O-3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Charleston and Goose Creek | SC259 | $2,385 / $1,941 | $2,652 / $2,223 | $2,721 / $2,529 |
| Beaufort and Parris Island | SC258 | $2,403 / $2,103 | $2,835 / $2,319 | $2,856 / $2,661 |

![Grouped bar chart comparing 2026 with-dependents BAH for E-5, E-7 and O-3 in the Charleston and Beaufort rate areas](/images/blog/naval-bases-in-south-carolina/south-carolina-navy-bah-2026.png)
*2026 monthly BAH with dependents near South Carolina's Navy sites. Source: Defense Travel Management Office BAH rate lookup.*

A few things stand out:

- Beaufort pays a bit more than Charleston at every rank shown. At E-7 with dependents, the gap is $183 a month.
- In Charleston, an E-5 with dependents gets $444 more a month than an E-5 without dependents.
- Goose Creek (ZIP 29445) and North Charleston (ZIP 29404) share the same rate area, SC259, so living closer to the gate does not change your BAH. Check your exact ZIP code with the DTMO tool.

BAH is meant to cover typical rent and utilities, not every home on the market. ## Living Near the Navy in the Lowcountry

**Schools.** Per a [December 2025 school information packet from NNPTC](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/NNPTC/SLPM_School_Information_Packet-Dec2025.pdf), Berkeley County School District serves Weapons Station housing. Marrington Elementary and Marrington Middle School of the Arts are on the base, and Goose Creek High serves grades 9 to 12. Dorchester District 2 covers much of Summerville. There are no DoDEA (Department of Defense Education Activity) schools here, so ask the School Liaison Office for help.

**Commutes.** Goose Creek, Hanahan and Summerville are popular because they are close to the Weapons Station gates. Traffic on I-26 can be slow at rush hour, so drive your commute before you buy.

**Flood risk.** Much of the Lowcountry is low and near water. Ask your agent about flood zones and flood insurance before you make an offer.

## Buying a Home Near South Carolina Navy Sites

**Use your VA loan.** A VA loan is a home loan backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs. Per the [VA's purchase loan page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-types/purchase-loan/), you can buy with no down payment as long as the price is not higher than the appraised value. There is no private mortgage insurance. If you receive VA disability pay, you do not pay the VA funding fee. Learn more in our [guide to the VA funding fee](/blog/understanding-the-va-funding-fee-a-complete-guide-for-military-families).

**Know the tax breaks.** Per the [South Carolina Department of Revenue's 2022 legislative update](https://dor.sc.gov/resources-site/lawandpolicy/Documents/Legislative%20Update%20for%202022.pdf), retirees can deduct all military retirement income from state tax, at any age. Veterans with a total and permanent service-connected disability may not owe property tax on their home and up to 5 acres, per the [SCDOR veteran exemption page](https://dor.sc.gov/tax-tips/veterans-learn-more-about-sc-property-tax-exemptions). See our guide to [South Carolina veteran property tax exemptions](/blog/south-carolina-veteran-property-tax-exemptions-2026).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a Navy base in South Carolina?

Yes, but not a stand-alone one. The Navy's main site is Naval Support Activity Charleston, the old Weapons Station, which is now part of Joint Base Charleston. The Navy also runs Naval Hospital Beaufort.

### Where is Navy nuclear power school?

Naval Nuclear Power Training Command is on Joint Base Charleston, in the Goose Creek area north of Charleston. It moved there from Orlando in 1998 and 1999.

### When did the Charleston Naval Base close?

The Charleston Naval Base and Shipyard closed on April 1, 1996, after being picked for closure in the 1993 BRAC round.

### What is the BAH for an E-5 in Charleston?

In 2026, an E-5 with dependents gets $2,385 a month in the Charleston rate area (SC259). Without dependents, the rate is $1,941.

### Is Joint Base Charleston Navy or Air Force?

Both. The Air Force runs the joint base, but the Weapons Station side is home to most of the Navy units in South Carolina.
