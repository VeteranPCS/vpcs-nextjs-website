For most of a military career, the hunt for a new home starts with one easy question: where is the base? Orders pick the city, and the family builds daily life around the installation. Retirement takes that starting point away. For many families, this last move, often called the final PCS (Permanent Change of Station), is the first time in 20 years or more that no one else picks the map.

That freedom raises a harder question. If the base is no longer the center of your life, what should be? This guide walks through a simple way to decide where to live after military retirement, using Orlando, Florida, as a working example. Orlando has strong veteran resources and a defense community inside a much larger civilian region. Whether it fits depends on what your household needs most, and the same steps work for any city.

The framework comes from Ted Moseley, a 20-year U.S. Navy veteran, Orlando REALTOR with Real Broker, LLC, and VeteranPCS agent. In his guide, [Is Orlando a Good Place to Live After Military Retirement? A Veteran's Local Guide](https://orlandonest.com/blog/is-orlando-a-good-place-to-live-after-military-retirement), Ted applies these questions to Central Florida's job centers, healthcare locations, housing costs, and everyday roads. His central idea is simple: decide what you want the next chapter to look like, then choose the place that supports it.

## Why the Final PCS Feels Different

On active duty, the installation was the fixed point on the map. After retirement, several things compete for that spot:

- A second career, or no career at all
- A spouse's job, which may now carry equal weight
- Where each person in the house gets regular healthcare
- Family you want to live closer to
- The full monthly cost of a home
- How you want a normal week to feel

There is no single right answer. The goal is to rank these for your household before you look at houses.

If the government is paying for your retirement or separation move, those household goods rules have their own deadlines and limits. Our guide to [final move rules for separation and retirement](/blog/final-move-entitlement-separation-retirement) covers the basics. Confirm your own entitlement with your transportation office before you set a closing date.

## Step 1: Start With Work, Yours and Your Spouse's

If either of you plans to work, the job location is a strong starting point, but only one. Two jobs may sit on opposite sides of a metro area.

In Orlando, different job centers can point you toward very different home searches:

- **Central Florida Research Park,** next to the University of Central Florida (UCF) on the east side, is home to Naval Support Activity (NSA) Orlando and many defense and simulation employers. Our guide to [moving to Orlando for a Research Park or defense job](/blog/moving-to-orlando-research-park-defense-job) compares the communities near it.
- **Lake Nona,** in southeast Orlando, is home to the Orlando VA Medical Center.
- **The Interstate 4 (I-4) corridor** runs through the region, with job centers such as Lake Mary to the north.

Ask a few plain questions. Is the job in the office every day, or hybrid? Is the offer firm? If one job is certain and the other is not, how much should the unknown job shape the search?

Then list the places each of you will drive to most often in a normal week, ranked by how often you go. Both spouses' routes matter more than any single address.

## Step 2: Map Healthcare Before You Map Houses

Healthcare is easy to overlook during a house hunt, yet for many retirees it shapes daily life more than any job. Start by confirming which care each person will actually use:

- **VA health care.** Eligibility depends on your service and discharge, and enrollment is a separate step. According to [VA.gov's eligibility page](https://www.va.gov/health-care/eligibility/), each enrolled veteran is placed in one of eight priority groups, which can affect how soon you are enrolled and what you pay.
- **TRICARE.** [TRICARE's retiring page](https://www.tricare.mil/LifeEvents/Retiring) explains that retiring from active duty is a Qualifying Life Event, and you have 90 days after your retirement date to enroll in a TRICARE plan. TRICARE Prime is only offered in Prime Service Areas, so your new address can affect your plan options.
- **Your spouse and family.** A spouse may have separate doctors or a civilian network. Their appointments count just as much as yours.

In the Orlando example, the [Orlando VA Medical Center](https://www.va.gov/orlando-health-care/locations/orlando-va-medical-center/) sits at 13800 Veterans Way in Lake Nona. It offers primary care, specialty care, and an emergency department open around the clock. The Orlando VA system also runs [clinics across the region](https://www.va.gov/orlando-health-care/locations/), including Clermont, Kissimmee, Deltona, Tavares, Viera, Palm Bay, Port Orange, and Daytona Beach.

So you do not need to live next to the main hospital. Find out where your regular appointments will really happen. Routine visits may be at a nearby clinic, while specialty visits may be at the medical center. Map both, plus your spouse's providers.

## Step 3: Decide How Much Military Community You Want

Some retirees want the commissary, the exchange, and active duty friends close by. Others prefer a civilian daily life with veteran ties. Both are fine. Just know which one you are.

Orlando is a good test of this question, because its military presence looks different from a typical base town. The [Navy's NSA Orlando installation guide](https://cnrse.cnic.navy.mil/Installations/NSA-Orlando/About/Installation-Guide/) describes a 40-acre facility inside Central Florida Research Park. It is home to the Naval Air Warfare Center Training Systems Division and the Army's Program Executive Office for Simulation, Training and Instrumentation. The same guide notes very limited support services on the installation. There is no commissary in Orlando. The nearest full-service commissary is at Patrick Space Force Base, about 54 miles away, and the Navy Exchange is near Orlando International Airport.

In other words, Orlando's military presence centers on training and simulation, not a large operational base that organizes the region. Some retirees want exactly that balance. Others will look closer to a larger installation.

If military services matter to you, list the ones you would really use and time the drive to each. For veteran support, the [Florida Department of Veterans' Affairs](https://www.floridavets.org/benefits-services/) offers free claims help through state service officers, and county veteran service offices offer free counseling too.

## Step 4: Build a Whole-Household Budget

The [Florida Department of Revenue](https://floridarevenue.com/faq/Pages/FAQDetails.aspx?FAQID=1466) confirms that Florida does not impose a personal income tax. That matters, but it is only one line in your budget.

Use current quotes for the actual homes you are considering:

| Cost item | Where to get the real number | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowners insurance | Quotes from insurers or an agent, for that address | Roof age, construction, and wind features can change the price |
| Property taxes | The county property appraiser and tax collector | Taxes can change after a sale, so do not rely on the seller's bill |
| HOA dues | The association's documents | Ask what the dues cover and about planned increases |
| CDD assessments | The sales contract and the county tax bill | Some newer Florida communities add these on top of county taxes |
| Maintenance | A home inspection and the home's age | Plan for roof, HVAC, and storm-related upkeep |
| Transportation | Your weekly drive list from Step 1 | Several major Orlando roads are toll roads |

A few Florida details are worth knowing:

- **Insurance.** The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation explains that a [wind mitigation inspection](https://floir.gov/consumers/wind-mitigation-resources) documents a home's storm-resistant features and may help you qualify for premium discounts. The Florida Department of Financial Services also publishes a [homeowners insurance toolkit](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/understandingCoverage/Guides) for shoppers. Our [hurricane prep guide for military families in Florida](/blog/hurricane-prep-military-families-florida) covers storm planning.
- **CDDs.** A Community Development District (CDD) is a special district that can fund roads, utilities, and amenities in some newer communities. Under [Florida Statute 190.048](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0190/Sections/0190.048.html), the first sale contract in a district must disclose that its taxes and assessments are in addition to county and other local taxes.
- **Exemptions.** Florida's homestead exemption and several veteran exemptions are real, but none are automatic. The Florida Department of Revenue's [property tax guide for military and veterans](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf) shows that most veteran benefits depend on factors such as a disability rating, and you apply through the county property appraiser. Do not build your budget on an exemption until you know you qualify. Our [Florida veteran property tax exemptions guide](/blog/florida-veteran-property-tax-exemptions-2026) explains each one.

## Step 5: Test an Ordinary Tuesday

A vacation shows you the best of a place. Before you commit, plan a scouting visit that tests a normal weekday.

On that visit, try to:

- Drive from a likely neighborhood to work or a regular appointment at 7:40 a.m.
- Drive the same route home in the late afternoon
- Find the VA clinic or doctor's office you would use most
- Check the grocery store, pharmacy, gym, and place of worship you would use
- Time the drive to family, or to the airport if you will fly to see them
- Walk through both spouses' routines, not just one

## Same Process, Different Answers

These planning examples are made up to show how the steps work. They are not real clients.

**A second-career household.** One spouse has a job offer near Research Park. The other spouse's work location is not settled yet. Should they buy close to the firm job now, or rent first until both jobs are known? Which areas still work if the second job lands across town?

**A healthcare-focused household.** One spouse expects frequent VA visits. The other spouse sees civilian doctors in a separate network. Where do those two sets of appointments overlap? Is a clinic close enough for routine visits, with the medical center reachable for the rest?

**A family-focused household.** The main goal is living near grandchildren. A lower-priced home farther away is tempting. Do the savings survive the cost of tolls, gas, and time? How often will they really make that drive?

Each could land in a different part of Orlando, or a different city, using the same process.

## Six Questions for Your Final PCS

Before you look at a single listing, sit down together and answer these:

1. Will either of us work, and where?
2. Where will each household member receive regular healthcare?
3. How important is being near military facilities and community?
4. Which people are we moving closer to?
5. What complete monthly housing cost fits our budget?
6. What should an ordinary Tuesday look like?

Once you have your answers, the search gets simpler. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Orlando a good place to live after military retirement?

It can be, especially if you want a large civilian economy, regional VA care, and defense-related jobs. It is not a traditional base town, so use the six questions above to decide.

### Do I need to live near the Orlando VA Medical Center?

Not necessarily. The Orlando VA system has clinics across the region, so routine care may happen closer to home. Find out where your regular appointments will be before you pick a neighborhood.

### Does Florida tax military retirement pay?

Florida does not impose a personal income tax, according to the Florida Department of Revenue. Federal tax rules still apply.

### Will I automatically get a property tax exemption as a veteran in Florida?

No. Florida's homestead exemption and veteran exemptions must be applied for through the county property appraiser, and many veteran exemptions depend on a disability rating or other conditions.
