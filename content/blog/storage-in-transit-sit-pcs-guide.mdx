Storage in transit is the military's temporary storage benefit, and for a lot of families it quietly becomes the most stressful part of a PCS. You land at the new duty station, you have not found a home yet, and your household goods are sitting in a warehouse on a clock you did not know was running. Storage in transit, almost always called SIT, gives you 90 days of storage at government expense. Miss the deadline and the bill shifts to you.

This guide covers how the 90-day window works, when you can get a second 90 days, what paperwork the extension takes, and how SIT connects to your house hunt.

## What SIT Is and Where It Happens

SIT is short-term storage that is part of your household goods shipment, authorized under [Joint Travel Regulations](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Policy-Regulations/Joint-Travel-Regulations/) paragraph 5236. The Joint Travel Regulations, or JTR, is the rulebook for military travel and transportation entitlements.

Your shipment can sit in storage at three points, and sometimes more than one.

- At origin, before it ships out
- At a point in transit between origin and destination
- At destination, while you look for or wait on housing

The Navy's [Storage in Transit briefing](https://www.navsup.navy.mil/Portals/65/HHG/Documents/DPS/sit.pdf) puts it plainly: SIT is used when you are in transit to your new duty station, or when you are looking for or waiting on housing. It is not authorized for short-distance local moves or for temporary duty moves.

One timing rule catches people off guard. SIT cannot begin before the date your household goods are released to a transportation service provider. The clock is tied to the shipment, not to your travel dates.

## The First 90 Days

Under JTR paragraph 5240, you are authorized 90 days of SIT. There is no charge to you inside that window.

If your goods are not removed from storage before the first 90-day period ends, and no additional SIT has been authorized, the storage charges become your responsibility. That is the whole risk in one sentence.

![Timeline of a military SIT window showing day 0 storage start, notices at day 45 and day 30 remaining, day 90 first period end, and day 180 extended limit](/images/blog/storage-in-transit-sit-pcs-guide/sit-timeline.png)

*The SIT clock, from the day your goods enter storage to the extended 180-day limit. Source: JTR 5240, 5242, and 5244.*

You will get warnings. The Defense Personal Property System, the online system that manages military moves, sends a SIT expiration notice to the email on record at 45 days and again at 30 days before expiration. Make sure the email in that system is one you actually read, especially if you are switching from a duty email to a personal address mid-move.

If you do not respond, the Personal Property Shipping Office will try to reach you, including by contacting your current command. If you still cannot be found or do not answer, the office may convert the shipment to your expense.

## Getting a Second 90 Days

When something outside your control keeps you from taking delivery, an additional 90 days can be authorized, for a total of 180. JTR paragraphs 5242 and 5244 list the situations that may qualify, decided case by case.

- Serious illness of the service member
- Serious illness or death of a dependent
- Impending assignment to government or government-controlled quarters, or privatized housing
- Being directed to temporary duty after arriving at the new permanent duty station
- Non-availability of suitable housing
- Awaiting completion of a residence under construction
- Acts of God, such as floods or earthquakes

Two of those are house-hunting reasons in plain clothes. "Non-availability of suitable housing" and "awaiting completion of a residence under construction" are the categories most homebuying families fall into.

There are limits on that generosity. Additional SIT may not be approved when you choose to have a home built while other housing is available, or when you choose to occupy privatized housing too small to hold all of your household goods. Choosing to wait is different from having no option.

## The Extension Paperwork

Extensions are not automatic and they are not granted after the fact. Per JTR paragraphs 4242-C and 5244-B, you must:

- Submit a written request
- Submit DD Form 1857, Temporary Commercial Storage at Government Expense
- Include supporting documents, such as a deployment letter or current orders with all amendments
- Get the request in before the first 90-day period expires

You can submit in person, by fax, or by email. The origin or destination Personal Property Shipping Office approves or denies on a case-by-case basis. The responsibility to submit on time is yours, and that is stated repeatedly in the official guidance for a reason.

If you are heading straight into a deployment after reporting, request the extension before you leave. Members can be held responsible for storage costs when an extension was not requested prior to deployment.

## SIT Versus Non-Temporary Storage

SIT and NTS are different benefits, and knowing which one you need can save you money and stress.

| | Storage in Transit (SIT) | Non-Temporary Storage (NTS) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Short-term, part of the shipment | Long-term storage |
| Standard length | 90 days, extendable to 180 | Long-term, tied to your orders |
| Typical use | House hunting, awaiting quarters | Overseas tour, long deployment |
| Governing rule | JTR 5236 | Requested through your shipping office |

![Side-by-side comparison of storage in transit and non-temporary storage showing purpose, length, and typical use](/images/blog/storage-in-transit-sit-pcs-guide/sit-vs-nts.png)

*SIT is a short bridge. NTS is a long park. Choosing wrong costs money.*

Under JTR 5252, SIT may be converted to NTS with approval from the Personal Property Office. You can request the conversion if you receive new orders, or if you deploy before taking receipt of your goods. For a deployment, the preferred approach is NTS at origin rather than SIT at destination, so talk to the origin office before your goods are picked up if you already know a deployment is coming.

## Getting Some of Your Things Early

You do not have to wait for the whole shipment. Under JTR 5246, you may be authorized one partial lot withdrawal and delivery from SIT. A second partial withdrawal may be authorized if unforeseen circumstances beyond your control create a hardship, such as a delay in projected quarters availability.

The practical tip: ask for partial delivery before your shipment is picked up or placed in storage, and mark the items you want pulled early on your inventory sheets. Requesting later is harder because nobody knows which crate holds the crib.

Be aware that if multiple withdrawals cost more than delivering the whole shipment at once, you can be charged the excess. Two trips is usually fine. Five is a bill.

## How SIT Shapes Your House Hunt

Here is the part most PCS guides skip. Your SIT window is a real deadline on your home search, and it should shape how you plan.

Ninety days sounds like plenty until you count backward. You need time to tour homes, get an offer accepted, complete a VA appraisal, clear underwriting, and close. In a normal market that stack can eat 45 to 60 days on its own, and that assumes you found the right house in the first two weeks.

Three moves keep you ahead of the clock.

- Use [house hunting leave](/blog/house-hunting-leave-pcs-guide) before or right after you report, so touring is not competing with in-processing.
- Get fully underwritten preapproval before you arrive, not a quick prequalification. It shortens the back half of the timeline.
- Tell your agent your SIT expiration date on day one, so closing timelines get built around it.

If closing is going to land past day 90, do not wait and hope. File the extension request with the reason that fits, most often non-availability of suitable housing or awaiting completion of a residence under construction, and file it before the window closes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many days of SIT do I get?

You are authorized 90 days under JTR 5240. An additional 90 days, for 180 total, may be approved case by case when conditions beyond your control prevent delivery.

### What happens if my SIT expires?

If your goods are not removed before the authorized period ends, storage charges become your responsibility. If the shipping office cannot reach you, it may convert the shipment to your expense.

### What form do I need for a SIT extension?

DD Form 1857, Temporary Commercial Storage at Government Expense, along with a written request and supporting documents such as current orders or a deployment letter, submitted before the first 90-day period expires.

### Can I get some of my belongings before the rest?

Yes. JTR 5246 allows one partial lot withdrawal and delivery, with a second possible under unforeseen hardship. Request it before pickup and note the items on your inventory.

### Does SIT apply to a local move?

No. SIT is not authorized for intra-city moves within the same duty station limits, or for temporary duty moves. It may be authorized for inter-city moves between residences in the same metropolitan area.
