BAH during a PCS move confuses a lot of service members, and the stakes are real. BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing) is often the second-largest part of your pay, and a PCS (Permanent Change of Station) changes it. For a typical move between U.S. duty stations, your old station's rate applies during travel, and the new rate starts on your reporting day. This guide walks through each stage of the move, plus advance BAH, rate protection and what happens if your family stays behind.

Your orders, dependents and housing situation can change which rules apply. Confirm your case with your finance office before you budget.

## BAH During a PCS: The Timeline

These four steps cover a typical move between U.S. duty stations. Overseas moves and special cases can follow different rules.

### Step 1: You get orders

Your BAH does not change when orders arrive. This is the time to plan your housing budget. Look up the rate at your new duty station with the [DTMO BAH rate lookup](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/BAH-Rate-Lookup/). You will need the ZIP code of the new base, your pay grade, and whether you have dependents.

If you need cash for a new lease, you can ask about advance BAH (more on that below). Our guide to [PCS advance pay](/blog/pcs-advance-pay-guide) covers the other advances you can request.

### Step 2: You leave your old base and travel

If your old permanent duty station (PDS) is in the United States, its locality BAH generally applies until the day before you report to your new station. This includes members without dependents leaving government barracks or dorms: old-station BAH starts when those quarters end. See section 10.10.2.1 and Table 26-38 of the [DoD Financial Management Regulation, Volume 7A, Chapter 26](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/documents/fmr/current/07a/07a_26.pdf).

BAH-T, also called BAH-Transit, is a location-independent allowance for certain travel situations, such as leaving an overseas OHA area. Living in U.S. barracks before a PCS does not, by itself, mean you get BAH-T. Members in initial training and those with dependents staying behind may follow separate rules. The [military compensation BAH page](https://militarypay.defense.gov/PAY/ALLOWANCES/bah.aspx) explains the allowance types.

Your travel pay, such as mileage and per diem, is separate from BAH. See our guide to [PCS travel pay, mileage and per diem](/blog/pcs-travel-pay-mileage-per-diem-2026).

### Step 3: You sign in at your new base

The new rate starts when you report for duty, not when you arrive in town or sign a lease. Under section 10.10 of the [DoD Financial Management Regulation, Volume 7A, Chapter 26](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/documents/fmr/current/07a/07a_26.pdf), eligibility at the new PDS begins on your reporting day. Ask finance to confirm that your recorded reporting date is correct; a processing delay does not change that effective date.

This matters for timing. If the new base pays more, signing in on time gets you the higher rate sooner. If it pays less, budget for the drop before you sign a lease.

### Step 4: You settle in

Once you are on the new rate, a few rules shape what you get next:

- **Rate protection.** If BAH rates drop at your location, you keep the higher rate as long as your status does not change. A PCS is one of the changes that ends protection, per the [DTMO BAH page](https://www.travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Basic-Allowance-for-Housing/). After you arrive, you get the same protection at the new location.
- **Government-owned housing.** Assignment to government family quarters generally ends locality BAH. Privatized housing works differently: you keep receiving BAH and use an allotment to pay rent to the housing company, per the [Air Force housing FAQ](https://www.housing.af.mil/FAQs/). Check which kind of housing you are offered.
- **Temporary lodging.** Temporary Lodging Expense (TLE) helps with eligible lodging and meal costs near your old or new duty station in the continental United States (CONUS). The standard limit is 21 days for CONUS-to-CONUS moves and moves from outside CONUS to CONUS, or 7 days before a move to an outside-CONUS station. Reimbursement depends on your family and local rates, with a $290 daily cap, per [DFAS TLE guidance](https://www.dfas.mil/militarymembers/travelpay/armypcs/tle/). It does not cover house-hunting trips. Our [TLE guide](/blog/temporary-lodging-expense-tle-pcs-guide) explains how to claim it.

Need help finding a home that fits your new rate? [Connect with a VeteranPCS agent near your next base](https://www.veteranpcs.com/contact-agent). There is no cost and no obligation.

## Advance BAH for a New Lease

Advance BAH helps you pay move-in costs like a security deposit and first month's rent at your new duty station. Per the [DoD Financial Management Regulation, Chapter 26](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/documents/fmr/current/07a/07a_26.pdf):

- You can get up to 3 months of your expected BAH.
- You usually repay it over 12 months, and the repayment can stretch in some cases.
- You should request it within 30 days of the expense.

If your landlord returns your deposit, you pay that part back right away. Advance BAH is for renting, not for buying a home, per the [Navy's advance BAH request form](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Reference/Forms/NPPSC/NPPSC7000-2_REV_04-2024.pdf). If you need to break your current lease to move, read our guide to [breaking a lease with military orders under the SCRA](/blog/breaking-a-lease-with-military-orders-scra).

## When Your Family Stays Behind

Sometimes the family cannot move right away. A spouse may have a job, or a child may be finishing the school year. You may still be able to get BAH at a rate that fits where your family lives.

**The family stability rule.** Federal law, [37 U.S.C. 403a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/37/403a), covers moves within the U.S. It applies from 180 days before to 180 days after your PCS. You may qualify if your spouse is working or in school, a child is in grades K-12, a family member is enrolled in the Exceptional Family Member Program (EFMP), or a family member has a chronic illness. Your service can then pay BAH at the new duty station rate, the old duty station rate, or your dependents' location rate.

**Navy old-station BAH.** Since October 2024, Sailors can ask to keep their old duty station rate while dependents stay near the old base, per the [NAVADMIN 192/24 fact sheet](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Messages/NAVADMIN/FACT_SHEETS/Fact_Sheet_NAV_192_24.pdf). You get only one housing allowance, and it ends if the family moves.

**Separate households.** In other cases, your service may approve a separate household through what the DoD calls the secretarial process. Ask your finance office or command early, since approvals take time.

## BAH and Overseas Moves

An overseas assignment does not always end BAH. Duty stations in Alaska and Hawaii still use BAH. At foreign duty stations, members renting private housing generally receive OHA (Overseas Housing Allowance), which covers actual rent up to a cap plus eligible utility and maintenance allowances, per the [DTMO OHA page](https://travel.dod.mil/Allowances/Overseas-Housing-Allowance). On an unaccompanied overseas tour, eligible members may receive BAH for dependents living in the U.S. and without-dependent OHA for their own housing abroad if government quarters are not provided, per the [military compensation BAH guidance](https://militarypay.defense.gov/PAY/ALLOWANCES/bah.aspx). Ask finance which rules apply to your orders and family location. Our [OHA guide](/blog/overseas-housing-allowance-oha-guide) covers how it works, and our guide to [what to do with your home when you PCS overseas](/blog/pcsing-oconus-what-do-i-do-with-my-home) helps with the house you leave behind.

## When 2027 BAH Rates Come Out

New BAH rates take effect each January 1. The 2026 rates were released on December 11, 2025, with an average increase of 4.2 percent, per the [Joint Base San Antonio repost of the DoD's 2026 BAH release](https://www.jbsa.mil/News/News/Article/4358569/department-of-war-releases-2026-basic-allowance-for-housing-rates/). As of November 2026, the 2027 rates had not been announced. Based on past years, expect them in mid-December. For a refresher on how rates are set, see [2026 BAH rates: what changed and how to check yours](/blog/2026-bah-rates-what-changed-and-how-to-check-yours).

## Turning Your New BAH Into a Housing Plan

Once you know your new rate, decide whether to rent or buy. BAH is tax-free and meant to cover typical rent and utilities in your area. Many military families use it toward a mortgage with a VA loan, which lets eligible buyers purchase with no down payment, per the [VA's purchase loan page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-types/purchase-loan/). To see what your rate could buy, read [how much house you can afford on BAH](/blog/how-much-house-can-you-afford-on-bah).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What BAH rate do I get while traveling during a PCS?

For a typical move from a U.S. duty station, old-station locality BAH applies through the day before you report. Members without dependents leaving U.S. barracks generally receive that locality rate once their government quarters end. BAH-T applies to certain other cases, including travel from overseas OHA areas; initial training and dependent-location rules can differ.

### When does my BAH change to the new duty station rate?

On your reporting day at the new duty station, under the governing DoD pay rules. Arriving in the area or signing a lease does not start the new rate. Confirm that finance records your reporting date correctly.

### Does rate protection carry over after a PCS?

No. A PCS ends rate protection at your old location. Once you are on the new location's rate, you are protected from future decreases there.

### Can I keep BAH at my old location if my family stays?

Possibly. For moves within the U.S., 37 U.S.C. 403a lets your service pay the old location rate in some cases, such as a spouse's job or a child in school. The Navy also has a specific old-station BAH request.

### How much advance BAH can I get?

Up to 3 months of your expected BAH, usually repaid over 12 months. It is meant for rental move-in costs, not a home purchase.
