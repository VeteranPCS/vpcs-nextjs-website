A common myth is that VA (Department of Veterans Affairs) home loans are only for active-duty troops and combat veterans. That is not true. National Guard and Reserve members can earn the very same benefit, including buying a home with no down payment. The catch is that the service requirements are a little different. This guide lays out exactly how a VA loan for National Guard and Reserve members works and how to prove you qualify.

If you drill on weekends and serve your country part time, this benefit may already be yours. Let's look at the rules.

## **The Same Benefit, Different Service Rules**

Once you are eligible, a Guard or Reserve member's VA loan is identical to an active-duty member's. You get the core advantages that make this loan special: no down payment, no monthly private mortgage insurance, and competitive rates. The [benefits of a VA loan](/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-a-va-loan) do not shrink because you served part time.

There is even good news on cost. Before 2020, Guard and Reserve members paid a higher VA funding fee than regular military borrowers. The [Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2019](https://www.benefits.va.gov/benefits/blue-water-navy.asp) ended that gap. Since January 1, 2020, everyone pays the same funding fee, which now depends only on your down payment and whether you have used the benefit before. Our [VA funding fee guide](/blog/understanding-the-va-funding-fee-a-complete-guide-for-military-families) explains those tiers and the exemptions.

## **National Guard Eligibility**

To qualify for a VA loan through National Guard service, you must meet one of these standards from the [VA](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/eligibility/):

- At least 90 days of non-training active-duty service under Title 10, or
- At least 90 days of active-duty service that includes at least 30 days in a row, with your discharge paperwork showing a qualifying activation, or
- 6 creditable years in the National Guard, and you are still serving, or
- 6 creditable years in the National Guard, and you were discharged honorably or placed on the retired list.

The 90-day paths generally apply to members who were activated, such as for a federal mission or deployment. The six-year path is the one that covers many traditional Guard members who never got called to long stretches of active duty.

## **Reserve Eligibility**

The rules for the Selected Reserve are very similar. You meet the minimum length of service if you served for:

- At least 90 days of non-training active-duty service, or
- 6 creditable years in the Selected Reserve, and you are still serving, or
- 6 creditable years in the Selected Reserve, and you were discharged honorably or placed on the retired list.

As with the Guard, the six-year path is what makes the benefit reachable for career reservists who drill regularly but were never mobilized for a long tour.

## **What "6 Creditable Years" Really Means**

A creditable year is a year of satisfactory service in the Guard or Selected Reserve, the kind that counts toward retirement. You generally earn it by drilling and training as scheduled and meeting your annual points requirement. If you have been a steady, drilling member for six years or more, you very likely meet this standard even if you were never activated.

If you are not sure where you stand, your unit's administrative office or your points statement can confirm your creditable years. That record becomes important when you request your eligibility document.

## **How to Prove Your Eligibility**

To use the benefit, you need a COE, or Certificate of Eligibility. This is the document that shows a lender you qualify. Guard and Reserve members usually prove service with records such as a Report of Separation and Record of Service (NGB Form 22) for the Guard, a points statement, or a DD214 if you were activated.

The easiest route is to let your lender request the COE for you through the VA's online system, which often returns it in minutes. You can also request it yourself. The [VA's how-to-request-a-COE page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/how-to-request-coe/) walks through each option. Once you have it, the rest of the process looks the same as it does for any VA buyer.

## **You Still Have to Qualify With a Lender**

Meeting the service requirement gets you the benefit, but a lender still decides how much you can borrow. Just like active-duty buyers, you need steady income, a reasonable credit history, and manageable debt. The home also has to appraise and meet the VA's basic condition standards.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

**Can National Guard members get a VA loan?**

Yes. National Guard members qualify with 90 days of qualifying active-duty service, or with six creditable years in the Guard while still serving or after an honorable discharge or retirement. The benefit is the same one active-duty members receive.

**Do reservists qualify for a VA home loan?**

Yes. Selected Reserve members qualify with 90 days of non-training active-duty service, or with six creditable years in the Selected Reserve while still serving or after an honorable discharge or retirement.

**Do Guard and Reserve members pay a higher VA funding fee?**

No, not anymore. Since January 1, 2020, Guard and Reserve members pay the same VA funding fee as regular military borrowers. The fee now depends only on your down payment and whether you have used the benefit before, and many disabled veterans are exempt.

**How do I prove my Guard or Reserve service for a VA loan?**

You request a Certificate of Eligibility, usually through your lender. Guard members often use an NGB Form 22 and a points statement, while activated members use a DD214. Your lender can pull the document through the VA's online system.
