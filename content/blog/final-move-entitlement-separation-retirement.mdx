Your final move entitlement is the last shipment the government pays for, and it does not work like the PCS moves that came before it. Where you can go, how long you have to go there, and how much storage you get all change the moment your orders say separation or retirement instead of reassignment.

Get this wrong and you are paying out of pocket at exactly the time your income is changing. Get it right and the government pays to put your household anywhere in the United States you decide to call home. That decision is a real estate decision as much as a logistics one, so it is worth understanding early.

## The Eight-Year Line

Everything starts with how long you served on continuous active duty.

**Less than eight years, separating.** According to [Move.mil](https://move.mil/moving-guide/retirees-separatees), the government will pay only to move your belongings to your home of record or your place entering active duty. You can still move somewhere else, but you cover any cost above what it would have cost to move your full weight allowance to that authorized point.

**More than eight years, or retiring.** The government may cover relocating you to a **home of selection**, anywhere in the United States, depending on your type of discharge. That is a much larger benefit than most people realize, and it is the one that shapes the house hunt.

You can still move outside the United States, but you pay the difference. The personal property office calculates the government's share using the cost of transporting your full weight allowance to the farthest possible point within the 48 contiguous states from your current station.

## The Deadline to Schedule

This is the single most common way families lose the benefit: they wait for life to settle and the window closes.

- **Separating with less than eight years:** schedule the move within **180 days** of your active duty release date.
- **Separating or retiring with more than eight years:** schedule within **one year** of your active duty termination date.

Extensions exist, but they are not automatic and they are not granted for preference or convenience. Move.mil lists three grounds: medical treatment, education or training you are pursuing to qualify for civilian employment, and unexpected events you can document, such as legal proceedings or a delay in selling or building a home. Requests usually go by email to your branch of service. Generally, extensions can be requested in short intervals for no longer than six years, though each branch varies.

If you do not know where you are landing yet, request the extension early rather than letting the clock run.

## You Only Get One Storage Option

On a normal PCS you can often use storage at both ends. On a final move, you pick one.

**Long-term storage at origin.** Non-temporary storage where you were last stationed, for up to **180 days for separating members** and **one year for retirees**. If you have not scheduled final delivery before that expires, the additional storage is your bill. Our [non-temporary storage guide](/blog/non-temporary-storage-nts-pcs-guide) covers what can and cannot go into the crate.

**Short-term storage at destination.** Storage in transit at your new location for up to **90 days**, for both separating and retiring members. Blow past 90 days and you pay the excess, and you may also lose the ability to file a loss or damage claim with your military claims office. The [90-day SIT rules](/blog/storage-in-transit-sit-pcs-guide) apply the same way they did on every other move.

Move.mil's own advice is direct: only use short-term storage at destination if you already know your final delivery address. If you are still house hunting, the 90 days will disappear faster than you expect.

## What Does Not Change

Three things carry over from every PCS you have already done.

**Weight allowance.** The amount you can move still depends on your final rank at separation or retirement. Check your figure in our [2026 weight allowance guide](/blog/military-pcs-weight-allowance-2026).

**Professional gear.** You can still move up to 2,000 pounds of professional books, papers, and equipment, and your spouse can still move up to 500 pounds.

**Shipping method.** You can let the government coordinate the whole move, do a [personally procured move](/blog/ppm-move-how-to-maximize-your-incentive-2026), or combine the two.

## What Does Change: Your Vehicle

This one costs real money and catches people off guard. When you separate or retire, you drive or ship your vehicle at your own expense. There are no mileage allowances, no per diem, and no inability-to-drive allowance for the vehicle on a final move.

If you are thinking about shipping a car overseas, budget for it twice, because import and customs clearance fees in many host countries are steep. Our [PCS vehicle shipping guide](/blog/pcs-vehicle-shipping-guide) covers what the military does and does not pay for on a normal move, and the gap here is the whole point.

## PPM Advances Are Limited by Branch

If you plan a personally procured move, the advance payment rules tighten at separation. Move.mil lists them by service.

| Branch | Advance for a final move |
|---|---|
| Army | None for separating members; retirees up to 60% of the 95% PPM incentive |
| Air Force | Retirees and separating members up to 60% of the 95% incentive |
| Marine Corps | Retirees and separating members up to 50% of the 95% incentive, with a weight estimate and an inspector visit |
| Navy | No advance for retirees or separating members |
| Coast Guard | Up to 60% of the 95% incentive while still on active duty; none after the termination date |

Plan your cash flow around that. A move you fund up front and get reimbursed for later is a different financial event than one the government books for you, especially in the months when pay is changing.

## The Short-Distance Move Out of Quarters

A useful rule most people never hear about. If you live in government or privatized housing and your agreement requires you to move out before you have chosen a home of selection, you are authorized a short-distance household goods move from those quarters to a local temporary residence nearby.

Two details make this valuable. The usual rank-based weight limits do not apply to that local move, and it does **not** count as your final move. You still need proper authorization and funding, and the paperwork differs by branch. The Air Force can arrange it on your separation or retirement order; the Army wants a memo from the garrison housing office with the funding citation.

## Where This Meets the Home Search

Home of selection is the reason this article lives on a real estate site. If you have more than eight years in, the government will move you to any point in the United States. That means your housing decision can be driven by cost of living, job market, family, and state tax treatment of military retired pay rather than by where the last set of orders happened to put you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do I have to use my final move after separating?

180 days from your active duty release date if you have less than eight years of continuous active duty; one year from your termination date if you have more than eight years or are retiring.

### What is a home of selection?

The destination a retiree or a separating member with more than eight years of continuous active duty may choose anywhere in the United States, with the government covering the move there depending on discharge type.

### Can I extend my final move deadline?

Yes, with documented cause such as medical treatment, education or training for civilian employment, or an unexpected delay like a home sale falling through. Extensions come in short intervals and generally cannot exceed six years total.

### Does the government ship my car when I retire?

No. On a final move you ship or drive your vehicle at your own expense, with no mileage or per diem allowance.

### Can I use both origin and destination storage on a final move?

No. You choose one: long-term storage at origin, or up to 90 days of short-term storage at destination.

## The Bottom Line

The final move is a benefit with an expiration date and a destination you get to choose.
