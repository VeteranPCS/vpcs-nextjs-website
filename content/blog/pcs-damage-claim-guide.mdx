The delivery crew pulls away, you open the third box, and the leg of your dining table is snapped clean off. A PCS damage claim is how the military lets you recover that loss, and it is one of the few parts of a move that pays you back for something going wrong. It is also one of the easiest benefits to lose, because the whole thing runs on two deadlines that start the day your shipment is delivered.

One of those deadlines changed in 2026. Shipments picked up on or after May 15, 2026 now get a full year to file, instead of nine months. This guide walks through what to do on delivery day, how to file in the Defense Personal Property System, what full replacement value really pays, and when to hand the file to your service's claims office.

## The Two Deadlines That Decide What You Get Paid

Almost every denied or underpaid claim comes down to missing one of these. They are separate, and they run at the same time from your delivery date.

- **180 days to give notice.** You tell the moving company, in writing, which items are missing or damaged.
- **9 or 12 months to file the itemized claim.** This is the detailed, item-by-item claim with values and photos.

According to [Military OneSource](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/resources/millife-guides/moving-claims/), if your shipment was picked up on or after May 15, 2026, you have up to 12 months from the delivery date to file the itemized claim. If it was picked up before that date, the older nine-month deadline still applies. File after your deadline and you are only eligible for depreciated value, not full replacement value.

The 180-day notice window applies to household goods shipments picked up on or after May 15, 2020, and non-temporary storage shipments picked up on or after July 1, 2021. Older shipments than that are held to the previous 75-day rule.

## What Full Replacement Value Actually Means

Your household goods are covered at full replacement value, often shortened to FRV, at no extra cost to you. That coverage is automatic. You do not buy it.

Full replacement value means the transportation service provider, or TSP, which is the moving company the government hired for your shipment, owes you the lesser of the repair cost or the replacement cost for each item. If a qualified inspector says a piece can be repaired for less than it costs to replace, the company may pay for the repair instead.

A replacement item has to be the same or a similar item. It can be new or used. The company will still ask you to show what the item was worth, what condition it was in, and what it costs to replace.

That last sentence is the reason the next section exists.

## Step 1: Document Everything on Delivery Day

Your claim is only as strong as what you can prove. Delivery day is your best and cheapest chance to build that proof.

- Take dated photos of any damage before the crew leaves, including the packing material it came out of.
- Check the inventory numbers against the boxes and note anything missing before you sign.
- Ask the crew for the Notification for Loss and Damage At Delivery form and fill it out with them.
- Confirm the moving company's contact information is legible on that form.
- Do not throw away a damaged item, and do not get it repaired, until the claim is settled.

If you shot before-photos during pack-out, this is where they pay off. A [PCS binder with your inventory and receipts](/blog/the-ultimate-pcs-binder-guide-get-organized-for-your-move) turns a frustrating negotiation into a short one.

## Step 2: Give Notice of Loss or Damage

Notice is the short version. You name each item, give its inventory number, and describe the loss or damage in a sentence. You do not need values or photos yet.

There are two ways to do it. You can sign the Notification for Loss and Damage At Delivery form on delivery day, or you can submit the Notification of Loss and Damage After Delivery form in the [Defense Personal Property System](https://dps.move.mil/cust/standard/user/home.xhtml), the same portal where you booked your move. Look for "Start My Loss and Damage Report."

You can send more than one notice inside the 180-day window. Every time you unpack another box and find something broken, add it.

## Step 3: File the Itemized Claim in DPS

This is the detailed filing, and it is where most of the work is. For each item, gather what you can:

- Manufacturer and model number
- A short description of the item
- The inventory item number from your shipment
- Purchase price and year of purchase
- A plain description of the damage
- A repair estimate, if you have one
- Photos and receipts

One exception worth knowing: non-temporary storage and direct procurement method shipments are not filed in DPS. Those claims go straight to the moving company by email, fax, or mail.

## What the Moving Company Owes You, and When

The moving company is on a clock too. These are the deadlines it has to meet for shipments picked up on or after May 15, 2020.

| Step | Company's deadline |
|---|---|
| Confirm it received your claim | 15 calendar days |
| Pay, deny, or make an offer (claims of $1,000 or less) | 30 calendar days |
| Pay, deny, or make an offer (claims over $1,000) | 60 calendar days |
| Respond to your counteroffer | 7 calendar days |
| Hire a repair firm | 20 calendar days from claim submission |
| Inspect the item being repaired | 45 calendar days |
| Pay or repair after you accept a settlement | 30 calendar days |

![Bar chart comparing moving company response deadlines for a military damage claim, showing seven days for a counteroffer, fifteen days to confirm receipt, thirty days for small claims, and sixty days for claims over one thousand dollars](/images/blog/pcs-damage-claim-guide/tsp-response-deadlines.png)

*What the moving company owes you, in calendar days. Source: Military OneSource, Understanding Moving Claims.*

The offer is a starting point, not a final number. You can counter on any item that is not marked final, and you should if the offer does not cover what the item actually costs to replace. Accept only when you are satisfied.

## Faster Options: Quick Claim and Essential Items

Two shortcuts are worth knowing about.

**Quick claim settlement.** If the damage is minor and the delivering company offers it, you can file paperwork on delivery day and get paid within five calendar days. The cap is $1,500 total for shipments picked up on or after May 15, 2022. You cannot later file on those specific items, but you can still file in DPS for anything else you find.

**Essential items.** These are the things you need for daily living: a refrigerator, a mattress, a washer and dryer, necessary medical equipment. Entertainment items do not count. Notify the moving company within seven days of delivery and it has two business days to respond. The fix might be a payment, a permanent replacement, or a rental while yours is repaired.

## When to Transfer Your Claim to the Military Claims Office

If you cannot reach an agreement, or the company has gone quiet for more than 30 calendar days, transfer the claim to your service branch's military claims office, or MCO. You have two years from the delivery date to do it.

Here is the trade. The MCO pays you the depreciated value up front, then keeps negotiating with the moving company on your behalf. If it recovers more, you get the difference up to full replacement value.

One warning: if the company has stopped communicating, do not dispose of, repair, or get an estimate on any claimed item without the MCO's approval first.

## Damage to the House Itself

Scratched floors, dented drywall, ruts in the lawn. That is a Real Property Damage claim and it runs on a much shorter clock.

Contact the moving company directly within **seven calendar days** of the last day its crew was at your residence. Walk the property with the crew before and after and write down what you see. The company may schedule an inspection within 15 calendar days. If you own the home, this matters twice over, because unrepaired damage shows up later in [a VA appraisal or a listing](/blog/rent-or-sell-your-home-when-you-pcs).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do I have to file a PCS damage claim?

Give written notice within 180 days of delivery. Then file the itemized claim within 12 months of delivery if your shipment was picked up on or after May 15, 2026, or within nine months if it was picked up before that date.

### Do I have to buy extra insurance for a military move?

No. Household goods, unaccompanied baggage, non-temporary storage, and direct procurement method shipments are all covered at full replacement value at no additional cost.

### What if I find damage after the movers leave?

Submit the Notification of Loss and Damage After Delivery form in DPS. You can file more than one notice inside the 180-day window as you keep unpacking.

### Can I file a claim on items I moved myself?

No. Items you move under a [personally procured move](/blog/ppm-move-how-to-maximize-your-incentive-2026) are your responsibility, which is one reason to think carefully about what you self-move and what you let the government ship.

### Who do I call for help with a claim?

The DPS Help Desk at 833-MIL-MOVE (833-645-6683), your local transportation office, or your service branch's military claims office.

Photograph everything, notice early and often, and do not let either deadline pass. The rules are written in your favor; the clock is not.
