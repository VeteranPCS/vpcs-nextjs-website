For many families, the hardest part of a PCS (Permanent Change of Station) move is not the packing. It is a spouse's job. Nurses, teachers, counselors, real estate agents and many others need a state license to work. Military spouse license transfer used to mean months of new paperwork, fees and sometimes new tests. Federal law has changed that for most licensed spouses. This guide explains the law, what to send, how compacts help, and how to get your costs paid back.

## Why Military Spouse License Transfer Matters

The numbers show why this is a big deal. Per the DoD's [2024 Active Duty Spouse Survey infographic](https://download.militaryonesource.mil/12038/MOS/Infographic/2024-active-duty-spouse-employment-and-education.pdf):

- The civilian unemployment rate for active duty spouses was 20%.
- 35% of spouses need a state-issued license for their job.
- 28% needed a new license or credential to work after their last PCS.

A second income also matters when you buy a home. Lenders look at steady income. A gap in work after a move can change how much house you qualify for. That is one reason to start on your license early.

## The Federal Law: 50 U.S.C. 4025a

The key law is part of the SCRA (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act). It was first added on January 5, 2023, and was rewritten in the FY2025 NDAA (National Defense Authorization Act) on December 23, 2024. You can read the current text on [Cornell's 50 U.S.C. 4025a page](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/4025a).

Here is the short version. If you or your service member move to a new state because of military orders, a "covered license" counts as valid in the new state. It covers the same scope of practice you had before.

### What Counts as a Covered License

Per the law, your license must:

- Be in good standing.
- Never have been revoked or disciplined by any state.
- Have no pending investigation for unprofessional conduct.
- Not have been given up while under investigation.

"License" is a broad word here. It includes a license, a certificate or other proof that you are qualified to practice.

### What Changed in 2024

If you read about this law in 2023, some rules have changed. Per a [Department of Justice letter to state licensing boards from December 2025](https://www.justice.gov/servicemembers/media/1421601/dl?inline):

- You no longer have to show you used the license in the past two years.
- Law licenses are now covered.
- Boards may not add extra requirements, such as asking for transcripts or test scores.

### What to Send the New State's Board

Per the [DOJ 2025 update on license portability](https://www.justice.gov/servicemembers/2025-update-portability-professional-licenses), you send the board in your new state:

- A copy of the military orders. A written notice from the commanding officer can also work.
- Your marriage certificate, if you are the spouse.
- A notarized affidavit. In it, you say your application is true, you will follow the new state's rules and scope of practice, and you are in good standing everywhere you hold or held a license.

**What if the board is slow?** Per the law, if a board cannot finish its review within 30 days, it may give you a temporary license. A temporary license has the same rights as a permanent one. Boards may also run a background check.

If a board will not follow the law, get help from your installation's legal assistance office. You can also file a complaint with the Department of Justice.

## Interstate Compacts: Check These First

An interstate compact is an agreement between states that lets a licensed worker practice in other member states. If your job has a compact and both your old and new states are members, the compact's rules apply instead of 4025a. Per the law, you cannot use the SCRA path for a license that already works across states through a compact.

The DoD's Defense-State Liaison Office keeps a list of [occupational licensure compacts](https://home.army.mil/stewart/5717/7435/8270/DSLO-FactSheet-ListOfOccupationalLicensureCompacts.pdf). As of January 2026, it named 19 compacts. They include:

- Nurse Licensure Compact, plus a separate compact for advanced practice nurses.
- Teaching (the Interstate Teacher Mobility Compact).
- Counseling, social work, psychology (PSYPACT) and school psychology.
- Physical therapy, occupational therapy, and audiology and speech-language pathology.
- Cosmetology, esthetics and massage therapy.
- Dentistry, dietitians, respiratory therapy, athletic trainers, EMS and physician associates.

The Nurse Licensure Compact is the biggest. It had 43 member jurisdictions in fiscal year 2025, per the [NCSBN annual report](https://www.ncsbn.org/public-files/fy25-nlc-annualreport.pdf). Some newer compacts are still getting started, so check whether yours is up and running in both states.

## Money That Helps Pay the Costs

**Relicensing reimbursement.** Each service pays back up to $1,000 per PCS for license or certification costs when you move to a new state. Per the [Military OneSource license transfer page](https://militaryonesource.mil/benefits/transferring-your-professional-license), this program was set up by Congress, and each branch runs its own. The Army also pays up to $1,000 per move for some business costs, per [MyArmyBenefits](https://myarmybenefits.us.army.mil/Benefit-Library/Federal-Benefits/Reimbursement-of-Qualifying-Spouse-Relicensing-Costs-and-Business-Costs?serv=120). You usually apply after you get the new license, so keep every receipt.

**SpouseWorks Scholarship.** This program used to be called MyCAA. Per [Military OneSource](https://militaryonesource.mil/benefits/spouseworks-scholarship/), it pays up to $4,000 toward a license, a certification, an associate degree or continuing education you need to keep a license. Spouses of all ranks now qualify.

**SpouseWorks career help.** SpouseWorks (formerly SECO) offers free career coaching and resume help. Its employer network, MSEP (Military Spouse Employer Partnership), has more than 1,000 partner employers, per [Military OneSource](https://www.militaryonesource.mil/resources/network/msep/).

Moving costs add up fast, so plan the whole budget. Our guides to [PCS advance pay](/blog/pcs-advance-pay-guide) and [PCS entitlements and benefits](/blog/prepare-to-pcs-understanding-your-pcs-entitlements-and-benefits) can help.

## What About Real Estate Licenses?

Real estate is a common job for military spouses. The federal law covers professional licenses broadly. Per the Department of Justice, all professional licenses qualify, though the law does not name real estate by name. Some states say so plainly. For example, [Colorado's Division of Real Estate](https://dre.colorado.gov/division-programs/real-estate-broker/licensing/military-credentialing) applies the law to brokers, appraisers and mortgage loan originators who move there on orders.

There is no interstate compact for real estate. Contact the real estate commission in your new state early and ask how it handles 4025a requests. If you are thinking about the field, read [how to become a real estate agent after the military](/blog/how-to-become-a-real-estate-agent-after-the-military).

## A Simple Timeline for Spouses

- **When you get orders:** check whether your field has a compact and whether both states are members.
- **Right after:** gather your orders, marriage certificate and license details. Get your affidavit notarized. Many bases offer free notary service at the legal office.
- **Before the move:** apply to the new state's board. Mention 4025a in your application.
- **After the move:** follow up if 30 days pass without a decision, and ask about a temporary license.
- **Once licensed:** file for your relicensing reimbursement with your branch.

Add these steps to your [PCS checklist and timeline](/blog/the-ultimate-pcs-checklist-and-timeline-for-active-duty-military-personnel) so they do not get lost in the move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do military spouses have to retest when they move to a new state?

In most cases, no. Under 50 U.S.C. 4025a, a covered license in good standing counts as valid in the new state when you move on military orders. Boards may not add extra requirements like test scores.

### Does the law still require two years of recent practice?

No. That rule was removed in the FY2025 NDAA, signed on December 23, 2024.

### How much does the military pay for spouse relicensing?

Up to $1,000 per PCS move for license or certification costs. The Army may pay up to another $1,000 for some business costs.

### What if my job has an interstate compact?

Use the compact. If your license already works in other states through a compact, the compact's rules apply instead of the federal portability law.

### Is MyCAA still available?

It has a new name. MyCAA is now the SpouseWorks Scholarship, worth up to $4,000, and spouses of all ranks qualify.
