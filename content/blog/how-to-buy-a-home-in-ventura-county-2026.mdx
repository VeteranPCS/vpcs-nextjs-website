## The Ventura County Market in Fall 2026

Ventura County is expensive, but it is not the frenzy it was a few years ago. According to the [California Association of REALTORS August 2026 report](https://www.car.org/aboutus/mediacenter/newsreleases/2026releases/august2026sales), the county's median single-family home sold for $925,000 in August 2026. That was down 1.3 percent from a year earlier. Homes took a median of 37 days to sell, and the county had 3.9 months of unsold inventory, up from 3.6 months a year earlier.

Mortgage rates matter just as much as prices. [Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) reported a 7.28 percent average 30-year fixed rate for the week of October 1, 2026. At these price levels, every quarter point changes your payment by a real amount, so get rate quotes in writing and compare them on the same day.

Conditions vary by neighborhood and price range. Ask your agent about recent sales before making an offer. For national context, see [is 2026 a buyer's market for military home shoppers](/blog/is-2026-a-buyers-market-for-military-home-shoppers).

## Step 1: Get Pre-Approved and Know Your Loan Limit

A pre-approval shows what a lender may lend after checking your finances. Include it with your offer.

### VA loans in Ventura County

The VA home loan is often the strongest option for eligible service members, veterans, and some surviving spouses. It allows no down payment and has no monthly mortgage insurance. Here is what to know for 2026:

- **Loan limits.** If you have full entitlement, [VA.gov](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-limits/) says you have no VA loan limit, as long as you can afford the payment and the appraisal supports the price. With remaining entitlement, the county limit helps set how much you can borrow without a down payment. Using a VA loan in the past does not always mean you have less entitlement; ask your lender to check your Certificate of Eligibility.
- **The 2026 Ventura County limit.** The [Federal Housing Finance Agency's 2026 county list](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/cll/fullcountyloanlimitlist2026_hera-based_final_flat.pdf) sets Ventura County's one-unit limit at $1,035,000. That is above the $832,750 national baseline but below the $1,249,125 high-cost ceiling.
- **Funding fee.** The VA funding fee is a one-time charge that keeps the program running. For a first-use purchase with less than 5 percent down, it is 2.15 percent of the loan, per [VA's funding fee page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/funding-fee-and-closing-costs/). Veterans who receive VA disability compensation, and active-duty members who show proof of a Purple Heart at or before closing, are among those who are exempt. Our [VA funding fee guide](/blog/understanding-the-va-funding-fee-a-complete-guide-for-military-families) explains the full table.
- **Credit scores.** VA does not set a minimum credit score, but lenders do. Many look for 620 or higher. Our [VA loan credit score guide](/blog/va-loan-credit-score-requirements) covers what lenders look at.
- **Occupancy.** You must plan to live in the home as your primary residence within a reasonable time after closing. If you are on active duty and cannot move in because of orders, [federal law](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section3704&num=0&edition=prelim) lets your spouse satisfy the occupancy rule.

For a deeper look at entitlement math, read [2026 VA loan limits explained for military homebuyers](/blog/2026-va-loan-limits-explained-for-military-homebuyers).

### Other loan options

- **FHA loans.** Backed by the Federal Housing Administration, these can allow 3.5 percent down with a credit score of 580 or higher, per [HUD's August 2026 handbook](https://www.hud.gov/sites/default/files/Housing/documents/40001-hsgh-Update-18.pdf). Lenders may set higher requirements. FHA loans carry mortgage insurance premiums for most borrowers.
- **Conventional loans.** These often need at least 3 percent down and charge private mortgage insurance below 20 percent down.

Shop at least two or three lenders. Each one must send you a Loan Estimate within three business days of your application, per the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-loan-estimate-en-1995/). Compare the rate, points, and lender fees line by line.

## Step 2: Check Down Payment and First-Time Buyer Help

Even with a no-down-payment VA loan, you will need cash for an earnest money deposit, inspections, and any closing costs the seller does not cover.

The [California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA)](https://www.calhfa.ca.gov/homebuyer/programs/va.htm) offers a CalHFA VA first mortgage for eligible buyers with a Certificate of Eligibility. CalHFA's [2026 income limit for Ventura County](https://www.calhfa.ca.gov/homeownership/limits/income/income.pdf) is $268,000. CalHFA programs require at least one first-time buyer to complete homebuyer education. CalHFA defines a first-time buyer as someone who has not owned and lived in a home in the past three years.

A few cautions:

- CalHFA's Dream For All shared-appreciation program only pairs with its own conventional loan, so it cannot be used with a VA loan. Its 2026 application window closed in March.
- Local down payment programs in Ventura County open and close as funding allows. Oxnard lists a [down payment assistance program](https://www.oxnard.gov/housing/affordable-housing) that is subject to funding. Ask your agent or lender which programs are taking applications when you are ready to buy.
- Always have your lender confirm that a specific assistance program can be paired with your loan type.

## Step 3: Hire an Agent and Find the Right Home

Since August 17, 2024, agents working with buyers through a multiple listing service must have a written agreement with you before you tour a home, according to the [National Association of REALTORS](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers). The agreement spells out what your agent will be paid, and that amount is negotiable.

VA buyers have a special wrinkle. VA rules have long limited the fees a veteran can pay. Under [VA Circular 26-24-14](https://www.benefits.va.gov/HOMELOANS/documents/circulars/26-24-14.pdf), which remains in effect until VA rescinds it, veterans may pay reasonable and customary buyer-broker fees. You can also ask the seller to cover your agent's fee as part of your offer. Read [why REALTORS still matter for military homebuyers](/blog/why-realtors-still-matter-for-military-homebuyers) for more on choosing an agent.

When you tour, focus on the roof, foundation, drainage, electrical panel, and heating and cooling. Drive your commute to the NBVC gate at rush hour before you fall in love with a house. If you are buying from another duty station, our guide to [buying a home sight unseen during a PCS move](/blog/buying-a-home-sight-unseen-during-a-pcs-move) shows how to do it safely.

## Step 4: Make an Offer and Track Your Contingencies

Many California offers use the California Association of REALTORS Residential Purchase Agreement. Your signed contract sets the deadlines for inspections, appraisal, and loan approval. A contingency is a condition that can let you cancel and protect your deposit under the contract's rules. Ask your agent to explain each deadline and how to use that protection. Mark the agreed dates on your calendar as soon as the offer is accepted.

### Key deadlines to track

- **Loan Estimate:** within three business days of your loan application.
- **Inspection, appraisal, and loan contingencies:** use the dates in your signed contract, including any changes you and the seller agree to.
- **Late Transfer Disclosure Statement:** three days after in-person delivery, or five after mail or electronic delivery, to cancel in writing under the state rule below.
- **Lead paint inspection:** a 10-day opportunity for most homes built before 1978, unless the parties agree to a different period or you waive it.
- **Closing Disclosure:** at least three business days before closing.

Tips for a strong but safe offer:

- Include your pre-approval letter and proof of funds for your deposit.
- Keep your inspection contingency. Inspections are where costly problems show up.
- Ask for seller credits toward closing costs if the home has been on the market a while. Our guide to [using seller concessions to lower your closing costs](/blog/how-to-leverage-seller-concessions-to-lower-your-closing-costs) explains how.
- If you are on orders, ask about a closing date that lines up with your report date.

## Step 5: Read California's Required Disclosures

California requires sellers to share a lot of information. Read every page, and ask your agent about anything unclear.

- **Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS).** The seller describes known defects and the condition of the home. If the TDS or a material change arrives after you sign an offer, [California Civil Code 1102.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1102.3) gives you three days after in-person delivery, or five days after mail or electronic delivery, to cancel in writing.
- **Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD).** Under [Civil Code 1103.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1103.2), this tells you whether the home sits in a FEMA flood zone, a dam inundation area, a high or very high fire hazard severity zone, a state wildland fire area, an earthquake fault zone, or a seismic hazard zone.
- **Lead-based paint.** For homes built before 1978, sellers must share what they know about lead paint and give you up to 10 days to test, per the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards).
- **Earthquake safety.** For many homes built before 1960, sellers must give you the Homeowner's Guide to Earthquake Safety under [Government Code 8897.1](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=8897.1).
- **HOA documents.** If the home is in a homeowners association, review the budget, reserves, rules, and any special assessments.

Fire maps changed recently. [CAL FIRE's Office of the State Fire Marshal](https://osfm.fire.ca.gov/what-we-do/community-wildfire-preparedness-and-mitigation/fire-hazard-severity-zones) released updated hazard zones for local areas in early 2025, so a home's fire rating may differ from what a past owner saw.

## Step 6: Line Up Insurance Early

Lenders require homeowners insurance before closing. In California, finding a policy can take longer than in other states, so start shopping as soon as your offer is accepted.

- **Fire coverage.** If standard insurers decline a home, the [California FAIR Plan](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/5-residential/Top10Tips_FindingResidentialIns.cfm) is the last-resort option. It covers fire and a few related perils, so many owners add a separate policy for theft, liability, and water damage. The California Department of Insurance [reported in July 2026](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2026/Insurance-surge-expanding-options-for-Ca.cfm) that several insurers had committed to expand in the state, so get multiple quotes.
- **Earthquake coverage.** Standard homeowners policies do not cover earthquake damage, per the [California Department of Insurance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/eq-ins.cfm). Your insurer must offer earthquake coverage, but buying it is your choice.
- **Flood coverage.** Most homeowners policies do not cover flooding, according to [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/). Check the address on the [FEMA Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/home), especially near the coast and rivers in Oxnard, Port Hueneme, and Ventura.

Get a quote before your contingencies end. A high premium can change what you can afford.

## Step 7: Appraisal, Underwriting, and Closing Costs

The lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home is worth the price. VA appraisals also check that the home meets VA's minimum property requirements for safety and soundness. See [what to expect from a VA loan appraisal](/blog/va-loan-appraisal-what-to-expect).

During underwriting, the lender verifies everything in your file. Do not change jobs, open new credit, or make large purchases until you close.

Closing costs vary, but plan for these:

- **Lender and title costs.** Origination, appraisal, credit report, title insurance, and escrow fees. Your Loan Estimate lists them. Our guide to [VA loan closing costs](/blog/va-loan-closing-costs-what-military-buyers-pay) shows which ones VA buyers can and cannot pay.
- **Transfer tax.** California law allows a documentary transfer tax when property changes hands. [Revenue and Taxation Code 11911](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=11911.) sets the county tax framework. Ask your escrow officer to calculate the charge for your address, check exemptions and local taxes, and confirm who pays it under your contract.
- **Property taxes.** Under Proposition 13, California's base property tax is 1 percent of assessed value plus voter-approved local charges, per the [California State Board of Equalization](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/pub29.pdf). A typical home purchase triggers a new assessed value and a supplemental tax bill. Ask the county assessor about any exemption that applies, and budget for that bill so it is not a surprise.
- **Insurance and reserves.** Your first year of homeowners insurance and an escrow cushion are usually paid at closing.

Veterans rated 100 percent disabled may qualify for a California property tax exemption. Read [California veteran property tax exemptions for 2026](/blog/california-veteran-property-tax-exemptions-2026).

You will receive a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing, per the [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-closing-disclosure-en-1983/). Compare it to your Loan Estimate and ask your lender about any change. Do a final walkthrough to confirm agreed repairs, then sign and get your keys.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the 2026 VA loan limit in Ventura County?

If you have full VA entitlement, there is no VA loan limit; your income and the appraisal set your ceiling. If you have reduced entitlement, VA uses the county's 2026 one-unit limit of $1,035,000 to figure how much you can borrow with no down payment.

### What credit score do I need to buy a home in Ventura County?

VA sets no minimum score, but most lenders look for about 620 for a VA loan. FHA allows 3.5 percent down with a 580 score. CalHFA's VA loan requires a 640 score. Requirements vary, so compare lenders.

### How much are closing costs in Ventura County?

It depends on your price, loan, address, and contract. Expect lender fees, title and escrow fees, prepaid insurance and taxes, and any transfer tax that applies. Ask escrow to confirm the tax and any exemptions. Your Loan Estimate gives your estimated costs within three business days of applying.

### Do I need earthquake or flood insurance in Ventura County?

Neither is usually required by law, but standard homeowners policies exclude both. Your lender will require flood insurance if the home is in a FEMA special flood hazard area. Earthquake coverage is optional but worth pricing.

### Can I buy a home before I arrive at NBVC?

Yes. Many service members buy from their current duty station using a local agent, virtual tours, and remote or power-of-attorney signing. A VA loan still requires that you or your spouse plan to live in the home.
