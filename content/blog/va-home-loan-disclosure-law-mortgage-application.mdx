Yes, a new federal law will put a VA home loan notice on the standard mortgage application. The [21st Century ROAD to Housing Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-119publ101), Public Law 119-101, became law on July 11, 2026. It tells the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) to require Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to add a military service question and this notice to the form: "If yes, you may qualify for a VA Home Loan. Consult your lender regarding eligibility."

The deadline for FHFA to act is January 11, 2027. Until the new form is in use, do not wait for the paperwork. Ask your lender directly whether you have been checked for VA loan eligibility and ask to see VA terms next to any other quote.

This guide explains what the law does, what it does not do, and how it fits a home search in Oklahoma City or anywhere else you PCS (Permanent Change of Station, the military term for an assigned move). It was written with Oklahoma City Realtor and Air Force veteran Terry "TJ" Courtney, who first raised the topic for his clients near Tinker Air Force Base. Legal details below were checked against the enacted law.

## Why a VA Home Loan Disclosure Law Was Needed

A VA home loan is a mortgage backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). It is open to many service members, veterans, and surviving spouses. Even so, a buyer can apply for a mortgage and never have the VA option come up. The loan officer may run conventional or FHA (Federal Housing Administration) numbers, and nothing on the form forces the VA question.

TJ Courtney sees that gap in his own work. In his words:

> "I've sat across the table from more than one veteran near Tinker AFB who had no idea they were leaving a no-down-payment loan on the table." (TJ Courtney, Realtor and USAF veteran, Oklahoma City)

He notes it is usually not bad intent. The process just never prompted the conversation. The new law is meant to close that gap on the application itself.

## What the ROAD to Housing Act Actually Changes

Two sections of the law affect military and veteran homebuyers. Some early summaries numbered them differently, so here is how they read in the [enacted text published by the Government Publishing Office](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ101/html/PLAW-119publ101.htm).

### Section 601: The VA Home Loan Notice

Section 601 is titled "Military Service Question." It requires the FHFA Director to make Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac add a disclosure on the Uniform Residential Loan Application (URLA). The URLA is the standard mortgage application most lenders use, also called Form 1003.

- **Where it goes:** directly below the military service question and above the signature line
- **What it says:** "If yes, you may qualify for a VA Home Loan. Consult your lender regarding eligibility."
- **Who sees it:** every applicant sees the question, and the notice speaks to anyone who answers yes
- **Deadline:** FHFA must act within 6 months of enactment, which is January 11, 2027

Section 601 also orders a study by the Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress's watchdog agency. Within 18 months of enactment, or by January 11, 2028, GAO must report to Congress on whether fewer than 80 percent of lenders using the URLA have added the notice.

### Section 603: The VALID Act

Section 603 is the Veterans Affairs Loan Informed Disclosure (VALID) Act. It does two things.

- **Moves the military service question.** The current URLA already asks about military service, but the question sits early in the form, in the borrower information section, according to the [current Fannie Mae Form 1003](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/7991/display). The law requires the question to sit above the signature line, with "Yes," "No," and "Prefer Not To Answer" choices.
- **Adds VA to an FHA disclosure.** Under [24 CFR 203.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-203/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR7fe3ecd504c1eac/section-203.10), FHA lenders must give some applicants a one-page "informed consumer choice" notice comparing FHA costs with similar conventional loans. The VALID Act adds a comparison with a VA-backed loan at prevailing interest rates.

That FHA comparison has limits. It applies only to FHA applicants who, in the lender's judgment, may qualify for a similar conventional loan. The law also says lenders do not have to determine whether you are eligible for any loan in that notice. It is a cost comparison, not a VA eligibility check.

## What the Law Does Not Change

This law is about disclosure. It does not change the VA loan program itself.

- It does not change VA loan entitlement, funding fees, or appraisal rules.
- It does not require lenders to approve a VA loan or to offer one.
- It does not create a side-by-side VA comparison for every borrower. The new VA comparison lives on the FHA notice, which only some FHA applicants receive.
- It does not replace your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), the VA document that shows you qualify.

## Why VA Terms Are Worth Comparing

The notice matters because VA loans carry real benefits. According to [VA's purchase loan page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-types/purchase-loan/), VA-backed purchase loans offer:

- No down payment, as long as the sales price is not higher than the appraised value
- No private mortgage insurance (PMI)
- Often better terms and interest rates than other loans from private lenders
- Limited closing costs, which the seller may pay

Most VA loans also carry a one-time VA funding fee, unless you are exempt. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When does the VA loan disclosure requirement take effect?

The law took effect on July 11, 2026, but the form change comes later. FHFA must require Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to add the notice by January 11, 2027. The enterprises then set the dates lenders must start using the new form. Until then, ask your lender about VA eligibility yourself.

### Was the ROAD to Housing Act signed by the President?

No. The bill was presented to the President on June 29, 2026, and became law without his signature on July 11, 2026, according to the Office of the Federal Register note on the [enacted law](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ101/html/PLAW-119publ101.htm).

### Does this law change how VA loans work?

No. It does not change VA loan entitlement, funding fees, or appraisal rules. It changes what the application and one FHA notice tell you, so you hear about the VA option before you commit to another loan.

### Does every lender have to show the VA notice?

The rule applies to the Uniform Residential Loan Application that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac maintain, which most lenders use. Congress built in a check: GAO must report by January 11, 2028 on whether fewer than 80 percent of lenders using the form have added the notice.

### Will my lender now tell me if I qualify for a VA loan?

Not automatically. The notice says you "may qualify" and tells you to consult your lender. The VALID Act also says lenders do not have to determine your eligibility for the loans in the FHA notice. Your COE is what confirms eligibility.

### How do I check my VA loan eligibility before I shop for a home?

Request your Certificate of Eligibility through VA.gov, through a VA-approved lender, or by mail. Have your discharge or separation papers, such as a DD214, ready. TJ recommends confirming eligibility before you start touring homes near Tinker so you know your financing options.
