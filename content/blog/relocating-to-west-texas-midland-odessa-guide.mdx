Relocating to West Texas, especially the Midland and Odessa area, means you will hear a lot about oil, wide-open spaces, wind, and Friday night football. All of that is true. But there is a lot more to living in the Permian Basin, and most of it matters before you pick a house.

## The Short Answer: What to Know Before Relocating to West Texas

If you only read one section, read this one:

- **Midland and Odessa are close, but they are not the same.** Pick the city, town, or rural area that fits your work and your family, not just the house.
- **Oilfield commutes are different.** Your company office address may not be where you actually work most days.
- **West Texas weather is hard on homes.** Look closely at the roof and HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) system, and get a quality inspection.
- **Plan for insurance and property taxes up front.** The sales price is only one part of your monthly cost.
- **A VA loan offer can compete.** It takes an agent and lender who know how to structure and explain it.
- **You can start your search before you arrive.** Remote planning works, especially if you are selling another home at the same time.

Whether you are leaving the military, taking an oil and gas job, or just looking for a new place to call home, here is what Rowdy tells his clients.

## Midland vs. Odessa: Close, But Not the Same

> Midland and Odessa are only about 20 miles apart, and thousands of people travel between the two every day. But don't make the mistake of thinking they're basically one big city.

In Rowdy's words, each city has its own personality, neighborhoods, schools, housing options, price points, and amenities. The smaller communities and rural areas around them give buyers more choices, especially if you want more land, fewer neighbors, or a different pace than city living.

That is why his first questions are not about which house you like best:

> I want to know where they're going to work, what kind of commute they're comfortable with, what's important to their family, what they enjoy doing, and what they actually want their day-to-day life to look like.

Finding the right house matters. Finding the right location matters just as much.

<Callout type="info" title="VeteranPCS note">
The two cities share a regional airport. The [Midland International Air & Space Port](https://www.flymaf.com/airport-information/) describes itself as located halfway between Midland and Odessa. If you are coming from another state, our guide to [buying a home after military service](/blog/buying-a-home-after-military-service) walks through the basics of a first civilian home purchase.
</Callout>

## The Oilfield Is Part of Life Here

You cannot really talk about West Texas without talking about oil and gas. The energy industry has a big effect on the local economy and the housing market, and it shapes a lifestyle that newcomers may not expect.

<Callout type="info" title="VeteranPCS note">
The Permian region of western Texas and southeastern New Mexico produced more crude oil than any other U.S. region in 2025, about 6.6 million barrels per day and 48% of total U.S. crude oil production, according to the [U.S. Energy Information Administration](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67404).
</Callout>

Rowdy works in oilfield sales in addition to real estate, so he sees the commute question from both sides. His advice: if you are moving for an oilfield job, do not assume you need a house five minutes from your company's Midland or Odessa office. Depending on your role, your actual work may take you all over the Permian Basin.

A normal commute for a traditional office job and a normal commute for someone working in the Permian can be two very different things. Ask your employer where you will spend most of your workdays before you decide where to buy.

## West Texas Homes Have Their Own Considerations

> Don't compare a West Texas house directly to one you owned in another state or even another part of Texas.

Every real estate market is different. When you look at a home here, look past the countertops, flooring, fresh paint, and listing photos. Rowdy's checklist:

- **Check the big-ticket items.** The roof and the HVAC system can be expensive to repair or replace.
- **Get a quality home inspection.** Understand the home's condition before you commit.
- **Ask questions.** Anything that could become an expensive problem later is worth asking about now.

West Texas weather can be tough on homes. Extreme heat, high winds, hail, dust, and the occasional storm that seems determined to test everything you own all take a toll.

<Callout type="info" title="VeteranPCS note">
Texas purchase contracts usually include an **option period**. The buyer pays the seller a small, negotiated, non-refundable option fee for the right to end the contract for any reason during that window and get the earnest money back, according to the [Texas Real Estate Research Center at Texas A&M](https://trerc.tamu.edu/article/option-period-basics-2360/). That window is when buyers typically schedule general and specialty inspections, like a roof inspection. Ask your agent how many option days to request.
</Callout>

## Budget for Insurance and Property Taxes From Day One

Weather is also why insurance costs and property taxes should be part of your buying decision from the start. As Rowdy puts it, the sales price is only one piece of what a home actually costs you each month.

Two things to check with an insurance agent before you make an offer:

- **Roof age and coverage type.** The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/replacing-your-roof.html) explains that some policies pay the full cost to repair a roof at current prices (replacement cost), while others pay less for an older or worn roof (actual cash value).
- **Wind and hail deductibles.** The same TDI guide says to confirm whether wind and hail damage has a different deductible than other claims. That number affects what you pay after a storm.

On taxes, the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/) notes there is no state property tax. Property taxes are set and collected locally, so the rate depends on where the home sits. If you live in the home, a residence homestead exemption lowers the taxable value for school district taxes, and veterans with a VA disability rating may qualify for more. Our guide to [Texas military and veteran property tax exemptions](/blog/texas-veteran-property-tax-exemptions-2026) covers the details.

## Veterans: Don't Overlook Your VA Home Loan Benefit

If you are a veteran or an eligible active-duty service member, your VA home loan benefit can be a very valuable option. Rowdy's advice is direct: do not automatically believe anyone who tells you a VA-financed offer cannot compete with conventional financing.

Every transaction is different. What matters is having a Realtor and lender who understand VA financing and know how to structure and explain the offer to the seller. Look at the whole transaction, not just the purchase price.

<Callout type="info" title="VeteranPCS note: what VA rules allow">
- **No down payment** is required as long as the sales price is not higher than the home's appraised value, according to [VA.gov](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-types/purchase-loan/).
- **Sellers can pay closing costs.** VA does not limit seller credits for a loan's normal closing costs, according to [VA's funding fee and closing costs page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/funding-fee-and-closing-costs/).
- **Seller concessions are capped at 4%** of the home's reasonable value. Concessions are extras like paying your VA funding fee, paying off a debt, or prepaying your homeowners insurance.
- **The funding fee** for a first-time use with less than 5% down is 2.15% of the loan amount. Veterans who receive VA disability compensation are generally exempt.
</Callout>

Depending on the property and the negotiations, seller concessions, closing cost credits, or other financing strategies can make a real difference in your monthly payment and the cash you need to close. Results vary by deal, and nothing is guaranteed, so run real numbers with a lender before you decide what you can afford. Our guide on [how to use seller concessions to lower your closing costs](/blog/how-to-leverage-seller-concessions-to-lower-your-closing-costs) explains the strategy, and [Texas VA loan benefits for military homebuyers](/blog/texas-va-loan-benefits-for-military-homebuyers) covers state-specific programs. ## You Don't Have to Be in West Texas Yet to Start Looking

If you are moving from another state, or even another country, you do not have to wait until you arrive to start your home search. Rowdy says the planning can begin long before the moving truck shows up:

- **Learn the areas** and how they differ.
- **Set realistic price ranges** for the neighborhoods you like.
- **Review current inventory** and commute times.
- **Narrow down homes remotely,** using video calls and live walkthroughs when it makes sense. A live tour gives you a better feel for a home than listing photos alone.

If you are selling a home somewhere else at the same time, starting early matters even more. Buying and selling at the same time can be done, but the contracts, financing, timelines, and contingencies need to work together. That is where a game plan matters.

<Callout type="info" title="VeteranPCS note">
In Texas, a purchase that depends on selling your current home is usually written with the [TREC Addendum for Sale of Other Property by Buyer](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/addendum-sale-other-property-buyer), a state-promulgated form. ## What Rowdy Tells People About West Texas

West Texas is not going to be everybody's cup of tea. Rowdy is clear that he will not sell anyone a fairy tale about perfect weather and mountain views:

> It's hot. It's windy. Things are spread out. And yes, you're probably going to get dust on something approximately five minutes after you clean it.

But in his view, West Texas has something that is harder to put on a relocation brochure: opportunity, hardworking people, communities where people still help each other, and plenty of room for someone willing to work hard and build a life.

For veterans, local VA care is part of the picture too. The [West Texas VA Health Care System](https://www.va.gov/west-texas-health-care/about-us/) runs the George H. O'Brien Jr. VA Medical Center in Big Spring and the Wilson and Young Medal of Honor VA Clinic in Odessa, serving veterans across 33 counties in west Texas and southeastern New Mexico.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far apart are Midland and Odessa?

About 20 miles. Many people live in one city and work in the other, and the regional airport sits halfway between them. Even so, each city has its own neighborhoods, schools, and price points, so compare them separately.

### Do I need to live close to my oilfield company's office?

Not always. Depending on your job, your daily work may be spread across the Permian Basin. Ask where you will actually work most days before you choose a neighborhood.

### Can a VA loan offer compete with conventional offers in West Texas?

It can. Every deal is different, but a VA offer presented clearly by an agent and lender who know VA financing can be competitive. VA allows sellers to pay normal closing costs and up to 4% of the home's reasonable value in concessions.

### What should I inspect on a West Texas home?

Focus on the roof and HVAC system, and get a quality general inspection plus any specialty inspections you need during your option period. Also ask your insurance agent how the roof's age affects your coverage and deductible.

### Can I buy a home in Midland or Odessa before I move there?

Yes. You can research areas, set a budget, review listings, and tour homes by video before you arrive. If you are also selling a home, plan the timelines and contingencies together from the start.
